### Serat ("Breaking Rules", CC BY 4.0)
- **Who:** the FMA album credits say "Produced by Vlas Presnetsov, artworks by Anna Repman" (his partner). Flow Sessions came out of a 20-day Advent 2023 challenge: one track and one painting a day, ranging from ambient to hip-hop to breakcore. So Serat is not a dedicated DnB act.

- **Size:**

  - Bandcamp: Serat has no page.

- **Active?** Serat has no new FMA release since 2023. Blear Moon is active: its YouTube channel is posting "Guided Levitation" full albums dated 2026 (ambient/drone), and its SoundCloud was last modified Nov 2025.
- **Contact:** the FMA form at https://freemusicarchive.org/music/serat/contact/, or Blear Moon's Bandcamp contact and Instagram @immersivelove.

- **Contact:** none found. The only route is the netlabel (on-mix.com, uploader address zerov@on-mix.com in the archive.org metadata), and it may be defunct.

- SoundCloud related tracks of cast heal "message not delivered", Pizza Hotline "LOW POLY ROMANCE", Shiny "Disc. No. 12" and "reflection", aak3, straw, Akiba and Nyteout, then a second hop from the best hits.

## Part 3: how these games got their music

### Neon White (Angel Matrix / Ben Esposito, 2022): one artist, commissioned
- **Who:** Machine Girl (Matt Stephenson) is the only artist. The music was released as two OST volumes, "The Wicked Heart" and "The Burn That Cures".
- **How he was found:** Esposito, a fan, cold-emailed him in 2020: "I'm working on an unannounced PC game right now, and I think your music has the perfect energy for it." He cited the album *Gemini* and the track "Cyan Hardcore". Stephenson said yes once he saw Esposito wasn't "just some kid in his bedroom".
- **How it was made:** about half the final tracks grew out of Stephenson's existing demos; the brief was "a lost PS2 or Dreamcast game".
- **Price:** no terms published.
- Sources: https://kotaku.com/neon-white-fps-soundtrack-nintendo-switch-machine-girl-1849119261 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neon_White ; https://www.nme.com/features/boss-level-machine-girl-neon-white-3266386

### Motorslice (Regular Studio, Brazil; publisher Top Hat Studios; May 2026): one artist, commissioned
- **Who:** Pizza Hotline alone (9-track OST, vinyl via Black Screen Records).
- **How he was found:** the studio approached him. In his words: "They got in touch and were like 'We like your music, can you make a soundtrack for us in your usual style?'"
- **The deal:** he writes freely to level footage and demos, and they give feedback. He calls it "mutually beneficial" because his artist profile is tied to the game, and "the soundtrack ... will be released as an album in its own right". So he keeps the album release. No fee was stated.
- Sources: https://killthedj.com/interview-with-pizza-hotline/ (2024-09-09) ; https://blackscreenrecords.com/products/motorslice ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorslice

### Easy Delivery Co. (Sam C., solo dev, 2025): two artists on radio stations, not a playlist
- **Who:** Sohaoying and Tore "pr0te" Lehmann (Denmark). Steam lists 21 original songs, "Lo-Fi to Jungle". pr0te did the 101.7 FM station (10 tracks). Sohaoying did the rest, plus the 6-track 104.9 FM jungle EP in the July 2026 EasyRally update.
- **Your guess was half right:** it does have several artists (two), but both were commissioned for the game, so it is not a playlist of licensed tracks.
- **How pr0te was found:** "One of my very good friends is part of the publishing team that works with the game, and he introduced me to the project and Sam C." Using several artists was the developer's choice: "It was Sam's idea to have more than one artist on the OST and to have some different styles of music."
- **Unknowns:** how Sohaoying was found, and any terms.
- Sources: https://nowplaying.cool/jungle-fits-the-ps1-aesthetic-quite-well-pr0te-talks-easy-delivery-co/ (2025-11-06) ; https://store.steampowered.com/app/3994280/Easy_Delivery_Co_Soundtrack/ ; https://torelehmann.bandcamp.com/album/easy-delivery-co-1017-fm-original-soundtrack ; https://nowplaying.cool/easy-delivery-co-gets-a-free-update-and-new-jungle-tracks/

### STRAFTAT (Lemaitre Bros = "the letriene brothers"): a compilation of 12 artists, licensed catalogue
Your clarification confirms "the letriene brothers" are the STRAFTAT developers: French brothers Sirius and Léonard Lemaitre, publishing as Lemaitre Bros.

- **It is a multi-artist compilation, as you thought:** the community wiki lists 50 tracks by 12 artists. Major Axis has 14; the rest are Accelio, Thought Forms, Tormund, Sawteeth, Lifelikemoviee, MIL3SPERHOUR, ROOXG, TONYMELODICS, Packback, CASTCADIA and hyperix.
- **Where the tracks came from:** nearly all of these artists are on the Jungle Fatigue Records compilations (Vol. 3, 2023, and Vol. 4, 2024, run by Sawteeth in Seoul). The music arrived in updates: "More than 30 fresh new songs" in the MUSIC UPDATE (Mar 2025), 8 more in 1.2.3, and "12 fresh new music tracks" in the Bazaar update (Nov 2025), which thanks "Major Axis ... for contributing so much". There are also "JF_" (Jungle Fatigue) maps. Major Axis's Bandcamp bio says his music is "featured in the video games Straftat and Axyz". Lifelikemoviee released a "-JF/Straftat- Jungle Mixtape".
- **Not found:** any public statement from the brothers on how they found the musicians or what they paid. Their Steam news (49 posts) and PC Gamer interviews only list the tracks.
- **Best reading (inference):** they licensed the existing catalogue of one jungle collective, through Major Axis and Jungle Fatigue, rather than commissioning a score. The game is free, so a revenue share is unlikely. Confidence on the identity: high. Confidence on the deal: low, since no terms were seen.

### Pattern across the four
Three of the four (Neon White, Motorslice, Easy Delivery Co.) **commissioned** music: either the developer cold-messaged an artist they already loved, or the publisher made an introduction. None published a fee. STRAFTAT is the outlier: it **licensed existing tracks** from one jungle collective.

The artist keeping the right to release the soundtrack as their own album seems to be part of the value exchange (Pizza Hotline says so outright, and Machine Girl self-released the OST).
