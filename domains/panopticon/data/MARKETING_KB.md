## 1. What makes a short get picked up, per platform

**Stated ranking signals**
- TikTok [V]: For You ranks on interactions, video info, weak device signals; finishing a video "would receive greater weight"; follower count and prior hits "are NOT direct factors" (newsroom + Creator Academy FAQ). Creator Academy: "keep an eye on your retention rate… The second most important metric is your engagement… watch time is more heavily weighted" [V]. "Hook viewers in the first three seconds" [V].
- Instagram Reels [V]: Mosseri, 2025-01-22 video: "The top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends… pay close attention to average watch time, likes per reach, and sends per reach"; "likes are slightly more important for connected content, and sends are slightly more important for unconnected content" (quoted by Social Media Today). Creator FAQ: "engagement per viewer… watch time, sharing, following, liking, commenting, profile visits"; "We recommend videos to unconnected audiences that are 3 minutes or less"; "Make sure the first 3 seconds of your reel are engaging"; reply to comments "within the first 7 days" [V]. Originality (2024-04-30): duplicates → only the original recommended; "no visible watermarks"; aggregator accounts (10+ reposts/30 days) removed from recommendations [V].
- YouTube Shorts [V]: Help Center defines "Stayed to watch: The percentage of times viewers stayed to watch past the initial seconds of a Short" and "Engaged views: How many times viewers stayed to watch past the initial seconds, not including any loops." Since 2026-08-24 "views are counted the moment a video starts to play across all formats." YouTube publishes no Shorts ranking weights [V, absence]; the "seed audience then expand" model is a Creator Insider interview (Todd Sherman) [L].

**First 3 seconds.** Now stated by TikTok and Instagram [V] (above); YouTube's metric is literally "past the initial seconds." Practitioner data agrees: "1 second to hook" (Zukowski), 130%-speed Matriarch cut [V]. Grönvall: "the first few seconds are very important… I often start out by saying blah blah blah *in my game* so they know that it's a developer of a game who's talking" [V].

**Length.** Best game TikToks in a 1,500-video/700M-view set averaged 25–28 s, ~15 s watch time, 37% completion (GameDiscoverCo) [V]. Zukowski: as short as possible, loop [V]. Bopl's 27 Shorts: 23–60 s, median ~41 s, all dev-voiced story (measured via yt-dlp) [V]. Reconcile: 10–15 s loop for a raw moment; 30–45 s only when a narrated story earns it.

**Watch time vs likes.** Likes/favorites correlate with views, comments moderately, shares not (GameDiscoverCo set) [V]. TikTok weights completion; Instagram weights watch time + sends; YouTube's headline metric is stayed-to-watch [V]. Optimize completion first; sends on Reels.

**Captions/text.** "Always caption" (GameDiscoverCo); Mortal Rite credits subtitles [V]. Instagram down-ranks majority-text reels (2023) [V]. Corner text only.

**Sound.** Trending audio helped FREERIDE; Chewa won with original audio [V]. Steam: assume silent [V]. Bopl's shorts are voice-first and still hit 23M [V]: voice is fine if the visual reads muted.

**Frequency.** Mortal Rite: 253 videos / 1.96 days, 126M views, 61,749 wishlists (~1 per 2,000 views) [V]. Grönvall: one short "every Friday… a whole day… probably a short work day" [V]. TikTok Creator Academy: "Post more often! The more videos you post, the more opportunities you give the recommendation system" — no number [V]; gaming guide: "twice a week or every other day," keep the schedule [V]. YouTube: "How often do you upload Shorts per day/week/month? Is this frequency sustainable?" [V]. Instagram: growing creators "post 10 or more reels per month" [V]. "1–4/day" and "first hour" appear only in third-party blogs [V, absence from official pages].

**Posting times.** Buffer TikTok (7.1M posts): Sun 9 a.m. best, evenings 6–11 p.m.; YouTube (1.8M): Shorts Fri 4–7 p.m., Thu–Sat; Instagram (9.6M): Wed/Thu 6–11 p.m. Sprout 2026: TikTok Mon–Fri 1–8 p.m., Sunday worst. Hootsuite: TikTok Thu 7–11 a.m. [all V]. They disagree; TikTok's own rule beats them: post "at most, two hours before the activity peaks" shown in Followers → activity [V]. Timing is second-order.

## 2. What indie game shorts that worked looked like

- **Bopl Battle** (Johan Grönvall, Zapray; 500K+ copies; ~500K subs) [V, podcast transcript + channel data]. Format: dev voice-over telling one true dev story, scripted, opening "in my game…", a curiosity hook paid off honestly ("I almost deleted this ability from my game, but a friend of mine had the most brilliant idea to fix it" → 23.3M views). Titles: "I had to delete macho-slime from my game," "Turning a game-breaking bug into a feature," "I added a DRILL to my game to fix a big issue," "Balancing my game in a fun way." 27 Shorts, 23 over 2M, Jun 2023–Jun 2025; 14 before the 2023-11-16 launch, a 12-short burst Sep 25–Dec 28 2023. Process: gather stories over years, one short per Friday, script first, test on TikTok first ("first couple of TikToks were pretty flat… then one got like 200,000 views… when I made something similar it would do similarly well"), then fire the proven ones on YouTube around launch: "the bad videos I didn't post, so now YouTube thinks my channel's really good." Wishlists: "probably all of it" from shorts; TikTok post = "boom spike" on the wishlist graph, Sodapoppin stream = "nothing"; launched with "probably 70,000, something like that… I could be making that up"; per-video: "Don't remember." Failure mode: "too technical… as if they had played StarCraft." Regional start: first video ~50 Swedish views; "you need a video good enough that it reaches America."
- **Tiny Glade** [V]: grayscale GIFs, dev hashtags, no name; mailing list 28% CTR; page launch 10K wishlists/24 h, 257K/3 months.
- **Mortal Rite** [V]: progression builds (8.5M views), "you suggested X so I built it," face on camera, subtitles, end-card wishlist animation.
- **Farewell North** [V]: 950K views/48 h; pinned video replies compounded reach.
- **FREERIDE** [V]: "this is the game" + Ghibli sound; 500 wishlists/day; 48% found it via Steam search (name must be searchable).
- **The Matriarch** [V]: loop in 10 s, 130% speed, one striking image.
- **Screenbound** [V]: "can it play Doom"; X beat TikTok; 50K+ wishlists.
- **YapYap** [V]: TikTokers "making content off our trailer"; 900K wishlists.
- **Content Warning** (Landfall) [V]: free for 24 h on 2024-04-01, 6.2M owners and 204K CCU in 24 h; 100K+ paid copies day one at $7.99; 2.2M paid in two months (Landfall on X / GamesRadar). Nylund: worked because "people woke up one day and… they're just bombarded with a bunch of footage." Requires a free-to-play launch and a clippable co-op premise; not our shape.
- **STRAFTAT** (Lemaitre Bros) [V, absence]: free base + $5 DLC, Overwhelmingly Positive 17K reviews, Next Fest Oct 2024 demo; no post-mortem exists, only a PC Gamer interview with no marketing numbers.
- **Peak**, **Secret Shuffle** [V]: streamer chaos / low-fi over polish; "hide your CTA."

**Patterns**: one true story with a hook; chaos moment; satisfying system; "I added X"; a single image. **Failed** [V]: reposted trailers; high production value; watermarked cross-posts; no bio link; jargon.

## 3. MrBeast principles that transfer

From the leaked production doc [V]; "first 3 seconds" and "click and the hold" are not in it [L]. Transfers: judge by AVD/AVP not likes (for shorts: stayed-to-watch and completion); "first minute has the most loss" → frame 1; "stop telling people what they will be watching and start showing them"; caption promise must be paid in-clip (Grönvall independently: "I don't click bait it… TikTok kids… are pretty keen on finding when you're being fake" [V]); re-engagement beats at ~10 s in a 25 s clip; wow = the thing only this game does; "endings must always be abrupt." Does not transfer: 13-min length, spectacle budgets, brand deals.

## 4. Steam

**Wishlists.** Popular Upcoming now needs ~100K; Personal Calendar Bronze 5K–32K, Silver 8K–60K, Gold 50K–120K, Diamond 90K–360K; calendar CTR 31–34% [V]. 7,000 = Bronze floor. Median no-following page: 111 wishlists/2 weeks [V]. Wishlists don't decay [V]. Grönvall: "I remember passing 7,000 being like, yes, that's what Chris told me to pass" [V].

**Next Fest** [V, Valve]: one entry ever; demo ≥2–4 weeks early; separate demo-launch email within 14 days. Feb 2026: 3,500+ demos, median ~200 wishlists; top 5% ~7,000. June 2026: 4,382 demos, top 10% ≥+121 followers. Predictors: pre-fest wishlists r=0.825; 2-week momentum r=0.61; "2,000+ before SNF"; demo→wishlist 19.3%; demo CCU × 3 ≈ launch CCU [V].

**Page assets** [V, Valve]: capsules 920×430/462×174/1232×706/748×896, text-free beyond title; ≥5 1920×1080 screenshots; trailer gameplay-first, silent, 6×1 s micro-trailer auto-cut. Capsule with person + object (Clanfolk 37K→85K) [V].

**AI disclosure.** Valve requires survey disclosure; adoption 21.2% (Feb 2026) → 26.5% (June 2026) [V]. Measured effects, all proxy and confounded: Game Oracle (9,879 2025 releases, F2P excluded): 4 vs 7 first-month reviews, 84.6% vs 88.3% rating, "around 52.6% fewer reviews" after controls, authors note AI "is used more often in projects already constrained" [V]. Sulka Haro (53,597 releases 2023–2026): AI-flagged 10.9%→19.9%→30.8% of releases but 10–27% of estimated sales; failed AI games 72% used it for visuals, successful ones for voice/localization [V]. Llama & Griffin (June 2026 fest): 1/10 most-played disclosed vs 26.5% pool; "No public dataset compares AI-disclosed and non-disclosed games on wishlists across a Next Fest" [V]. Conclusion: disclosure correlates with ~half the reviews; visible AI art is the penalized case; no wishlist-level causal study.

**Page timing** [V]: post when genre, style, three environments, capsule, gameplay trailer exist; ≤1 month wait for a showcase slot.

## 5. Testing framework

**Unit**: one variable per week, 5–7 clips, mirrored natively to all three platforms, no watermark. Order: (1) first frame, (2) 10 s raw vs 35 s narrated story, (3) text overlay, (4) original vs trending audio, (5) slot ≤2 h pre-peak vs Buffer evening, (6) CTA visible vs hidden.

**Measure (labels verified unless marked)**
- TikTok Studio → Analytics → Content → tap video: "Retention rate," "Average watch time," "Watched full video" (finish rate), traffic sources, viewer info; Overview → "Traffic source," "Search queries"; Followers → activity by hour [V, Creator Academy 2026-08-18].
- YouTube Studio → Analytics → Content → Shorts: key metrics (views, likes, subscribers), "How viewers find your Shorts," "How viewers engaged" = "Stayed to watch"; Reach tab: "Stayed to watch," "Engaged views," "Average view duration" [V].
- Instagram reel → View insights: Views, Reach, Watch time, Average watch time, Skip rate (leave within 3 s, replaced view rate Aug 2025), Retention chart, Interactions (Likes, Comments, Shares, Saves), Profile activity, Follows [L: help.instagram.com errors; labels from Metricool/Babbleboxx].
- Steam (once live): UTM per platform; wishlists/day.

**Primary metric**: 3 s hold = TikTok retention at 0:03, Shorts "Stayed to watch," Reels 100−skip rate. **Secondary**: completion ("Watched full video"), then sends (Reels), likes/saves (TikTok), follows, link clicks. Views are output.

**Decision rule**: a variant wins only if it beats control on 3 s hold *and* completion across ≥3 clips (median). If a clip exceeds 5× channel median, freeze the format and post 3 near-copies within 7 days (Zukowski; Grönvall's "make something similar" [V]). Kill formats under median completion two weeks running. Keep a proven-clip catalogue for page launch and Next Fest; Grönvall's move — test on TikTok, replay only the winners on YouTube in the launch window — is the catalogue's purpose [V].

## 6. Regime recommendation for Ryan

**Month 1 (no Steam page)** — find the hook, build a mailing list/Discord. Don't burn the name.
- **TikTok**: 1/day, 6 days/week, posted ≤2 h before the Followers-tab activity peak (fallback 7–9 p.m. local until the tab has data). Mix: 35% satisfying-system (one clean shot from the tower, ragdoll), 25% chaos (pile-ups, breach), **25% Friday story-short in Grönvall's form** — scripted, dev voice, opens "in my game…", one true problem→fix story, 30–45 s, hook paid honestly, no jargon — 15% comment-reply videos. Raw clips 8–15 s, loop, corner text, muted-legible.
- **YouTube Shorts**: same clips, 5/week, Thu–Sat 4–7 p.m.; the Friday story-short always; watch "Stayed to watch" not views (views now count at play-start).
- **Reels**: 4/week (≥10/month per Instagram), Wed/Thu 6–9 p.m.; no watermark, never majority-text; ask for sends; reply to comments within 7 days.
- **Bluesky/X**: 1 GIF/day, engine hashtags, Tiny Glade route.
- CTA: "follow to see the pit finished" + mailing list; measure follows and signups. Bank every story as it happens (Grönvall: "gather those up over years") so the launch-window burst has 10+ proven shorts.

**When the Steam page exists** (≤2 months): CTA "wishlist, link in bio," 3 s end-card; launch page with a showcase + name reveal; UTM per platform; expect ~111 wishlists/2 weeks from cold, so month-1 audience is the point. Demo Nov–Dec 2026, Next Fest Feb 2027 as the second jump: ≥2,000 wishlists pre-fest, two-week creator/clip surge before it. **Launch window (Mar–Apr 2027)**: replay the top TikTok-tested shorts on YouTube near-daily for two weeks, Bopl-style. Post-fest keep the winning format 4–5/week; wishlists don't decay. Disclose AI honestly; keep AI out of visible art, the one measured penalty.
