## The ~$40 course
It is **BiteMe Games' "Steam Announcement Kit"**, which costs **$39** at https://biteme01.gumroad.com/l/steam-announcement-kit. It has 25 ratings, all 5 stars.
- It includes a press release template, validated contacts for 30+ press outlets, contact details and guidelines for the major trailer aggregators, short-form formats and a campaign calendar.
- **Recommended paid companion to this list.** Its public page doesn't name any of its contacts, so use it to cross-check this list and fill gaps, especially the trailer aggregators.
- Other candidates were checked and ruled out:
  - Chris Zukowski's How To Market A Game masterclasses cost far more and include no list.
  - Terminals.io is a paid PR distribution service run by Evolve PR. Third-party reports put it at roughly $150 to $3,000.
  - The Lince Works list doesn't exist publicly.
  - The Dutch Game Garden / Udemy PR course costs €69.99 and has no list.
  - Superstring's marketing manual costs $6.99 and has no list.

## Sources (free first)
- **Free:**
  - Outlet tips and news-desk addresses and named-writer emails, from contact pages, mastheads and author pages. GamesRadar+, PC Gamer, RPS and Indie Games Plus publish staff emails.
  - YouTube About pages and channel descriptions.
  - Steam Curator Connect (inside Steamworks). It sends copies straight to curators, so it's a later tool for builds; for the announcement, curators who feature upcoming games just need the store page.
  - Games Press free tier: one announcement a month, submitted by email. The paid Elite tier gives direct upload.
  - IMPRESS (impress.games) free tier.
  - PixelProspector's journalist list (old, no longer updated).
  - The itch.io community database of indie YouTubers.
  - r/gamedev press-list threads.

- **Paid:**

  - Agencies' influencer lists.

## Tools to sign up for now vs later
- **Now (announcement):** Games Press free submission, IMPRESS free press kit / newsroom, and a presskit() page on your own site.
- **Later (demo or playtest beat; these are key-distribution tools):**
  - Keymailer: free plan capped at 10 network requests a month; paid plans run about $25 to $99 a month.
  - Lurkit: freemium tier with one key campaign; indie discounts.
  - Woovit: free for creators, with free and paid tiers for developers. It can also collect interested creators before keys exist.

## How and when to pitch the announcement
1. **Have these ready before any email goes out:**
   - A live (or embargo-ready) Steam page.
   - An unlisted YouTube trailer link, plus a downloadable MP4 with no watermark.
   - A one-page press kit: logo, 6 to 8 screenshots, GIFs, a 100-word fact sheet, price ($7-10), and "friends-only online, one sniper vs many runners, N64/GameCube look".
2. **Send under embargo 5 to 7 days ahead.**
   - Subject line: `[EMBARGO <date> <time> UTC] PANOPTICON: reveal trailer`.
   - Name the embargo time in the first line of the email.
   - Pick a Tuesday to Thursday, around 14:00 to 16:00 UTC, so it lands on both UK and US news shifts.
   - Offer one exclusive first look, for example to Time Extension or PC Gamer, a day or two earlier if they want it. Don't offer it to more than one outlet.
3. **Go live** at the embargo time with the Steam page, trailer and press release. Send it through Games Press the same day. Post to X, Bluesky and Reddit.
4. **Follow up once,** 2 to 3 days later, with any traction (wishlists, view counts). Then stop.
5. **Pitch trailer aggregators and showcases on the day,** using the trailer file and Steam link: GamersPrey, Gematsu, Indie Games Hub, Best Indie Games, IndieGameJoe, and the Future Games Show / PC Gaming Show forms.
6. **Hold the let's-play creators for the demo beat,** for example Steam Next Fest on Feb 22 to Mar 1, 2027 (register by Jan 10, 2027). That's when Keymailer, Lurkit and Woovit come in.
