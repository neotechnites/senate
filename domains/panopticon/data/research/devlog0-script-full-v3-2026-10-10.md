  **4. PREMISE AND TITLE CARD**

PANOPTICON.
  (invincible style title card or a different intro to the name)

  For those who don't know, a panopticon is an architectural design for a prison, The idea is that you have a tower in the middle that can see every cell, but from the cell's perspective you can never truly know if a guard is watching you. 

(original panopticon drawing)

  What this does is create the feeling, for all prisoners, of constantly being watched, whether they are or not, letting just a few guards staff big prisons. 

(some other shot of a panopticon representing this)


  Which, if you think about it, is actually exactly how prisons work today; just instead of doing this through architecture, it's done through technology, with cameras.
(picture of a prison gaurd at security cameras, Perhaps an in engine recreatin of this)
  

  PANOPTICON is a first-person asymmetric multiplayer game about escaping a prison built so one guard can see everyone. One friend sits in the tower with a sniper rifle; everyone else runs the ring for the exit, and the other prisoners aren't your team. You sprint between cover, shove each other into the line of fire, and whoever makes it out takes the rifle and the tower.


(more shots of gameplay)

  

  **5. HINGE**

  

  Now, that sounds like exactly the kind of game I'm about to complain about. A little party friendslop game. You play it for three hours with your friends, you laugh, and you never open it again. And that's the problem. Because I think that's what indie multiplayer has become, and I think it loses that absolute magic of needing to spend real time in a game to play all its content and unlock all its content. Nowadays, all content is available from being opened, and you get through all of it in three hours.

  Now was the moment of truth. It was time to test it with a real human person. So I got on a call with my buddy so we could test it, and lo and behold: it all worked, first try. The rounds worked, the map looked awesome, it was fun, the design was perfect, and not one networking bug.

(gameplay, cut to black right at the end)
   
   Okay, obviously, that's not what happened at all.



  So, first, when he booted it up, the basic menu I set up for someone to host and someone else to join didn't fit in his view right. It was too big, and he wasn't even able to click the buttons he needed to join my match. So I fixed that and sent him a new build. Now, this

  time, we were actually able to get in a match together, and we were able to see each other and run around. Then we actually tried playing a match. And, let's say it, it didn't go completely smoothly.

  

  The build I'd sent him didn't have a rifle in it. Not his, not mine. The exporter had quietly dropped the gun out of the match scene when it packed the build, so we were two guys in a prison with nothing to shoot each other with.

  

  Once that was fixed and we did have a sniper, when I died, he didn't see me die. He saw me fall over face first and then just… lie there, face-planted, for the rest of the round. On my screen I was dead and watching the death camera. On his, I was a guy taking a nap on the

  ground. The bodies I was sending him had no idea whether they were on the ground or in the air, so they never left the death pose.

  

  When he died, nothing happened. No death screen, no death camera, no footsteps the whole time either, and if he hit Play Again it wiped his match while mine kept going.

  

  The last thing before we could actually play rounds was when he reached the portal that was supposed to make you the sniper for the next round: nothing happened.

  (each bug shown as it happened, from his point of view, recreated from the Sept 11 build and captioned as such: the oversized menu, the match with no rifle, the face-plant corpse, the client dying with no death screen, Play Again wiping his match, the portal doing nothing)
