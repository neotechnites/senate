# Shorts reference: "100+ indie game TikToks" study (Ryan's standing reference, 2026-10-12)

Ryan: "forever use this video as reference when coming up with shorts ideas." Full transcript pasted by Ryan 2026-10-12 (video title not given). Key points, in the video's order:

- **Viral = 100k+ views.** Gameplay is king. Unique look, mechanic, UI or design matters more than genre. Visual distinctiveness of 3-4/5 or more converts best.
- **Conversion:** about 1M views from gameplay gives ~290 Steam followers, roughly 2-2.5k wishlists (~2% of views to wishlists). Funny, devtip and relatable-dev videos get big views but convert a quarter to a tenth as well. Mr Moo: 2M views on a meme video brought ~80 wishlists, while 500k views on a 5-years-of-progress video brought ~1,000.
- **Templates that worked:**
  1. Pure gameplay ("15 seconds of our indie game"). Any length works, including 2 minutes.
  2. The trailer reposted vertically ("have you seen our trailer?"), using a blurred-bar background.
  3. **Progress over time** (the author's favourite): "5 years ago...", "week 1 / 1 month / now", "how it started vs how it's going". It makes people root for you.
  4. Showing a bug, or fixing one (real or staged).
  5. Answering negative comments, real or staged ("it's just a Fortnite ripoff" got 800k). Underdog effect.
  6. Adding things to the game from comments (the "sword holding a sword" video). It drives comments.
  7. Early game vs late game.
  8. A ridiculous or stupid mechanic or trick shot ("this will never fly", felling a tree with a body).
  9. Asking for feedback ("which one do you like?").
  10. Comparison to popular games as a mash-up (X meets Y). Beware looking like a copy.
  11. Nostalgia (a mechanic that recalls a childhood game; the Mario & Luigi one got 1.2M).
  12. Milestones such as a demo, a launch or a feature.
- **What didn't matter:** trends, CapCut, music, length, posting frequency, saying "wishlist" in the video (the bio link is enough). Paid boosts converted poorly.
- **Lessons:** test each style several times. A personal solo-dev account helps. Cross-post to Shorts. Curator TikTokers (Brax finds games, Mad Morph) can make a game blow up.
