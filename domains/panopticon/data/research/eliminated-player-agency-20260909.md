# PANOPTICON — Eliminated-Player Agency
### Research brief, 2026-09-09

**The constraint being solved, verbatim:** *"if they are shot, i want them to be able to have some kind of goal and effect, i hate multi player games make people sit our. think crawl and how it masterfully handles the asymetircal multiplayer."*

**Hard rule:** in the canonical mode a shot prisoner does **not** become a second shooter.

**Game shape assumed:** 3D first-person, Godot, Steam 2027-04-01. One shooter in a central tower with a hard information advantage; 1–7 unarmed prisoners running a ring course from start to end; cover and traps; rounds likely 2–6 minutes; optimised for 1v1–1v3; the match must produce exactly **one** winner.

This brief presents options and tradeoffs. It does not pick one.

---

## 0. THREE THINGS TO SETTLE BEFORE PICKING A MECHANIC

### 0.1 Does the shooter seat rotate?

Almost every option below gets cheaper, safer and better if it does. Crawl's dead-player loop is usually described as "you possess the monsters." That is the *content*. The *structure* is five properties, and the structure does the work:

1. **The role you lose is temporary by construction.** Hero is a seat, not a status.
2. **Your own progression persists across role changes.** Dying costs tempo, not equity — "This level carries over too, so if a hero is killed and become a Phantom, they'll have the same level and equipment if they manage to become a hero again" ([Use a Potion](https://www.useapotion.com/2018/01/crawl-review/)).
3. **A short, legible, earnable path back to the winning seat** — land the killing blow.
4. **Time out of the seat makes you stronger**, scaled to how well the current leader is doing (Wrath is earned when the opposing Hero levels up — [Crawl Wiki](https://crawl.fandom.com/wiki/Monsters)).
5. **Zero dead time.** Even while the hero shops, ghosts fly around collecting coins to cash for weak ooze minions ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/party-game-where-your-friends-are-the-dungeon-monsters-1794177023)).

If PANOPTICON's shooter seat rotates (everyone takes a turn in the tower; the match resolves on aggregate score), a shot prisoner is eliminated from a **round**, not from the **match**. The problem shrinks from "keep a player engaged for the rest of the match" to "keep them engaged for ≤3 minutes, and make sure their round still counted." That is the problem Crawl actually solved. If the shooter is fixed for a whole match, only the return-to-play options really work.

### 0.2 Where exactly is the "not a second shooter" line?

This ruling needs one more click of precision before the shortlist can be read cleanly, because the single best-evidenced pattern in the entire corpus sits right on it. Bomberman's **Revenge Cart** puts an eliminated player *outside* the arena wall where they can throw bombs in, and *returning them to play if they get a kill*. Konami's own manual: "Players who have died become Revenge Carts. Revenge Carts can throw bombs to attack other players from outside the Outer Wall Blocks… If they successfully defeat the players inside, Revenge Carts can return to the stage as players" ([Super Bomberman R2 manual](https://www.konami.com/games/bomberman/r2/manual/en/switch/page08.html)).

So: is "a dead prisoner who can *harass the tower* and win their way back" a second shooter, or not? It is not a rifle and it is not hunting other prisoners — but it is an attack. Options #1 and #2 below both assume this is **allowed**. If it is not, both drop several places and the brief's centre of gravity moves to #3 and #4.

### 0.3 The strongest argument against this whole direction, which should be confronted first

Behaviour Interactive has repeatedly refused to give dead Dead by Daylight survivors anything, and their reasoning applies to PANOPTICON directly. In a game with out-of-band voice, **the dead player already has maximum agency**; adding in-game agency mostly formalises an exploit and widens the gap between friends-on-Discord and strangers. Their community forum lays out both objections — SWF (premade-squad) amplification, and the killer's reward for a kill evaporating ([BHVR forums](https://forums.bhvr.com/dead-by-daylight/discussion/461608/would-it-be-such-a-terrible-idea-to-give-dead-survivors-something-to-do)). A since-banned player describes the existing leak in his own words: "I die early… I goto the end screen… I then tell him all the killer perks being used. I then spectate against the remaining players," providing "a remaining play-by-play of the entire match" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/381210/discussions/0/1709564118763121942/)). BHVR's answer to an absent player is a **bot replacement**, not empowerment, and in 7.2.0 they went further and stopped disconnected players spectating at all.

The counter-argument, from the opposite camp, is the one Siege players make: you cannot prevent it, only relocate it, and relocating it to Discord penalises solo queue. Both positions are defensible. Pick one deliberately.

## 1. THE GOVERNOR TAXONOMY

Every shipped instance of real eliminated-player power has a governor. Seven distinct types appear across ~60 games, ranked by how well they held. Read this before the shortlist — it is the toolkit each option is assembled from.

| # | Governor | Where it works | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Re-entry ramp** | Bomberman's Revenge Cart; Crawl's killing blow; Halo Infection / Zombie Panic as the degenerate case | Gives the dead player a self-interested goal. **The only governor that removes MOTIVE rather than throttling RATE — and therefore the only one that also solves kingmaking.** Nothing else in the corpus does this. |
| 2 | **Reputation coupling** | Trouble in Terrorist Town — the prop-possession punch-o-meter *cap* scales with the player's karma (`ttt_spec_prop_maxbonus 16` / `maxpenalty -6`, roughly Score/2) | Automatic, moderator-free griefing throttle. Unique in the corpus. |
| 3 | **Budget conservation** | White Noise 2 — "Ghosts can now break a single sigil, **if they didn't break one before dying**" | Post-death capability is drawn from your *unspent living budget*, so total round capability is constant. Elegant, cheap, and it appears exactly once. |
| 4 | **Sensor restriction, not channel restriction** | CS `mp_forcecamera 1`; Siege's fixed cams vs. driveable drones; Phasmophobia hiding D.O.T.S./shapeshift from observers; White Noise 2 hiding Scarecrows | **The rule that survived every test: restrict where the dead can LOOK, leave the talking alone.** |
| 5 | **When-not-where** | TowerFall's Trigger Corpses — your corpse detonates on your input, but only where you died | You choose the timing, never the position. The cleanest primitive in the corpus and trivially implementable. |
| 6 | **Rate limit / stock cap / cooldown** | Party Animals (start 3 coins, cap 5, +1 per 20s; banana 2 / projectile 3 / bomb 4); TTT punch-o-meter; R.E.P.O.'s battery; Unfortunate Spacemen's 15s light flicker; Project Winter's 75 Ghost Power + 180s | Throttles rate, never motive. **Always needed a toggle on top.** |
| 7 | **Toggle** | Party Animals host disable; Project Winter (disable-abilities, then removed from Normal); TowerFall opt-in variants; Damned allow-spectator; Escape the Backrooms difficulty tiers; TTT convars; Ubisoft pulling Gameplay After Death outright | **Every studio that shipped real dead-player power also shipped a way to turn it off.** Ship the toggle in the same release — the absence of one is why Siege's version was killed rather than made optional. |

**Plus an eighth, which is not a throttle but a kill switch held by the opposing side:** in the Town of Us Among Us mod, the **Phantom** wins by completing all tasks as a ghost — "**If you're clicked on, you turn into a ghost**" — and the **Haunter** loses its role if an Impostor clicks it ([role list](https://gist.github.com/ladyisatis/92164880511bd9568e81e894c28996e5)). This is the only design found where dead-player agency has **direct, real-time counterplay by the opposing team.** For PANOPTICON it is unusually apt: it converts the ghost from a balance problem into something the shooter can shoot.

### Three cross-cutting laws

1. **Eliminated-player agency must not scale with the number of eliminated players.** Among Us's Guardian Angel is the proof: GA count scales with deaths, so the losing side's power grows exactly as they lose — "It's impossible to win as impostor with that many dead people able to protect the living" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/945360/discussions/0/3463850618797381795/)). The same reasoning drove Ubisoft's "horde of drones" fear. **At 1v7, PANOPTICON has six ghosts by the end of a bad round. This law is the binding constraint on every option below.**
2. **Both failure modes live on one dial.** R.E.P.O. is the clean demonstration: tighten the ghost's battery and players report "spending more time waiting for it to recharge than actually using it"; loosen it and you get griefing. There is no setting that eliminates both. Choose which one you would rather have.
3. **Content does not cure ghost boredom; round length does.** Project Winter shipped teleport, four AoE abilities, an inventory slot, items, bonus challenges, global-event voting *and* a playable bear form, and still draws "**Being a ghost is too boring, and matches take too long**." Contrast White Noise 2 and Midnight Ghost Hunt's ~4-minute phases, which draw almost none of it. **PANOPTICON's 2–6 minute round is already most of the way to the answer — and any mechanic that lengthens rounds is partly undoing its own fix.**

Ranked for fit to *this* game's shape. Each: how it works, where it is proven, its failure mode, Godot cost, and what a headless bot match could measure.

### #1 — THE HAUNT: a leashed spirit that fights the tower, with an optional way back in

**How it works.** A shot prisoner becomes a spirit bound to the ring — intangible, unable to touch a living runner, and *leashed* (it stays near the action the way Crawl's ghosts are confined to the hero's room and the screen edge). It acts only through **pre-authored fixed course fixtures**: a light bank, a steam vent, a shutter, a fan, a loose gantry. Possession is a **commitment** — in Crawl, once a spirit possesses a spawner or trap "they cannot leave the form until they die or kill the main character" ([Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/dungeon-game-friends-control-monsters/)) — so you spend your turn on one choice, the possessed fixture is *visibly* possessed, and the shooter can shoot it to cost you that turn (the Town of Us counterplay principle). Every effect is shaped **against the tower**, never *for a named runner*. Ramp it with a Wrath-style accrual so a long-dead ghost is more dangerous than a fresh one. **Optional bolt-on, and the single highest-leverage decision in the brief:** add a **re-entry ramp** — a ghost who lands a meaningful hit on the tower buys their way back into the run. That is the Bomberman configuration, and it is the only known governor that removes the kingmaking motive rather than throttling it.

**Proven in.** **Crawl** ([Powerhoof interview](https://powerupgaming.co.uk/2015/11/19/developer-interview-dungeon-crawling-with-powerhoof/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crawl_(video_game)), [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/293780/Crawl/)). **Bomberman's Revenge Cart / Bad Bomber** — ~30 years across every Bomberman generation, from Super Bomberman 3 (1995) to Super Bomberman R2 ([Konami manual](https://www.konami.com/games/bomberman/r2/manual/en/switch/page08.html), [R Online manual](https://dds.konami.com/games/manual/sbro/multi/en/pc/page04.html), [openretro on SB3](https://openretro.org/snes/super-bomberman-3)); its longevity is the strongest indirect evidence in this brief, and there is essentially **no player criticism of it, positive or negative — it is simply accepted.** **Trouble in Terrorist Town** prop possession, governed by a karma-scaled meter ([TTT settings](https://www.troubleinterroristtown.com/config/settings/)). **TowerFall Ascension's** three opt-in death variants ([Variants](https://towerfall.wiki.gg/wiki/Variants)).

**Failure mode.** Four, in order of severity.
(a) **Ring geometry silently converts "environmental" into "targeted."** Runners on a ring are strung out along it. Cutting the lights at the 200m mark helps whoever is at the 200m mark. Kingmaking in an environmental costume, and the biggest risk in this option. TowerFall's **when-not-where** primitive is the cheapest defence: let the ghost choose *timing*, never *position*.
(b) **It scales with the number of dead** — Law 1. At 1v7 the sixth ghost breaks it. A shared ghost pool (one action budget split across all ghosts) is the standard answer.
(c) **Stalling.** Crawl is the corpus's only clear citation for this as a distinct failure mode: reviewers name "opportunities for players to hold up the game." When the dead have real agency they also acquire the power to hold the game hostage.
(d) **Visual chaos.** Crawl's action "can also get a little too chaotic when three players are controlling monsters" ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/games/crawl/b/switch/archive/2018/01/11/teach-your-friends-to-kill-each-other.aspx)). PANOPTICON's shooter is doing precision target ID down a scope; noise is not a neutral cost to them.

**Godot cost: MEDIUM.** The interaction is trivial; the netcode is not. You need a `Possessable` interface on hand-authored fixtures, arbitration for who possesses what, and an authority model. Keep it **server-authoritative with intent RPCs** rather than transferring `set_multiplayer_authority` per node — per-node authority transfer works in Godot 4, but every transfer is a bug surface. Most of the real cost is fixture authoring and tuning, not code.

**A headless bot match can measure.** Marginal effect size (same seeded round, haunting on vs off — Δ≈0 means decorative, Δ large means trivialising). **Kingmaking exposure, quantified:** for each ghost action compute Δ P(win) per living runner; if one action reliably moves one specific runner far more than the field, the mechanic is aimable. Simultaneous stimuli in the shooter's frustum per second, by dead count. Fixture-possession distribution (are 3 of 20 fixtures doing all the work?). Whether a ghost can unilaterally stall the round.

### #2 — THE RETURN: one rationed, self-earned way back into the run

**How it works.** A shot prisoner is not out; they are *pending*. They get exactly one chance to re-enter and must earn it themselves — not be rescued by a teammate. Shapes: a short solo gauntlet in a side space (Gulag-shaped), an in-world ritual on the ring in the intangible state (reach a marked point unspotted), or the Bomberman shape (land a hit on the tower). They re-enter behind, at the last checkpoint, stripped. The shooter gets counterplay: the re-entry is **visible and loud**, the way Apex's Mobile Respawn Beacon is visible on every player's minimap.

**Proven in.** **Warzone's Gulag** — Amos Hodge (Raven): single-life modes are "unforgiving and can encourage an overly cautious playstyle"; on refusing to make it teammate-dependent, "there is a certain poetry to a player earning their own way back into the game," because "not all players have a full squad working together as a well-oiled machine"; on the loadout, "enough so the player wasn't defenseless, but wasn't so strong that the Gulag player could drop onto active players for easy kills," and symmetry was a hard requirement ([TheGamer's writeup of the GameSpot interview](https://www.thegamer.com/warzone-devs-reveal-how-they-thought-up-the-gulag/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_of_Duty:_Warzone_(2020_video_game))). **Bomberman's Revenge Cart** (above). **Crawl's** killing blow. **Friday the 13th's Tommy Jarvis** — one scarce, *upgraded* re-entry slot ([wiki](https://fridaythe13th-game.fandom.com/wiki/Thomas_%22Tommy%22_Jarvis)). **Super Mario Bros. Wonder's** Ghost form is the gentle version — the ghost self-rescues by touching a live player or a player-placed **Standee**, on a 5-second timer that **shortens with each rescue** ([Ghost form](https://www.mariowiki.com/Ghost_(form)), [Standee](https://www.mariowiki.com/Standee)); the wiki's own summary of why Nintendo replaced the NSMB bubble is the thesis of this option: "**Players actively participate in their recovery rather than passively waiting for rescue.**" The negative control is teammate-gated revival: Fortnite's Reboot Vans launched to documented apathy — "random teammates simply didn't bother" ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/the-good-and-bad-of-fortnites-new-respawn-system-1833951661)).

**Failure mode.** It attacks the shooter's only verb. The shooter's entire skill expression is the elimination; if the elimination undoes itself, the shooter has nothing. Ubisoft's guardrail is the one to steal verbatim: **staying alive must remain strictly better than dying**, so "careless fights early in the round" never become viable ([Y6S2 Designer's Notes](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-gb/game/rainbow-six/siege/news-updates/76KxHcLFOo2PdhSS4t1HmZ/y6s2-preseason-designers-notes)). Second: the returned player defects — Friday the 13th had to restructure XP "to discourage Jarvis players from escaping on their own." Third: it lengthens rounds, which fights Law 3. Fourth, if death stops mattering entirely, all tension has to be carried by the objective clock — Last Year and Depth both landed there, and both are now delisted.

**Godot cost: MEDIUM to EXPENSIVE.** The in-world ritual is medium (a marked volume, an intangible pawn, respawn plumbing, one new match state). The Gulag version is expensive: a second small level, a concurrent match state, and a rule for what happens when the main round ends while you are still in there.

**A headless bot match can measure.** Shooter win-rate delta at each count with return ON vs OFF. Median dead-time per player. What fraction of returns actually succeed (a re-entry nobody wins is a punishment, not a mechanic). Round-length inflation. Whether dying early on purpose is ever positive EV — a directly simulable exploit check.

**Kingmaking exposure: lowest of the shortlist.** A player playing for their own re-entry is self-interested by construction. This is Crawl's real trick, and Bomberman's: the dead cannot *pick* a winner, they must *steal* the win.

### #3 — THE BANKED RUN: death ends your round, not your match, and dead time buys your next one

**How it works.** Presupposes §0.1's rotation. Two parts. (a) **Retained stake:** a shot prisoner still shares in the prisoners' round outcome, so they are still rooting rather than idling. (b) **Wrath:** while dead you accrue a currency whose accrual is *scaled to how well the current shooter is doing* — the better the tower plays, the more the dead bank — and that currency buys your next round (start position, a tool, a head start, a shot-tolerance).

**Proven in.** **Crawl's Wrath**, which Kotaku describes precisely as a resentment-prevention device: monster players who don't land the killing blow are compensated with an upgrade currency "giving you a better chance at success on the next round," and the transparency of that "prevents resentment among non-human players" ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/party-game-where-your-friends-are-the-dungeon-monsters-1794177023)); evolutions cost 10 / 25 / 40 ([Crawl Wiki](https://crawl.fandom.com/wiki/Monsters)). **Retained stake:** *Blood on the Clocktower* — "If you are dead, you still participate in the game, you may still talk, and you still win or lose when your team wins or loses" ([official rules](https://wiki.bloodontheclocktower.com/Rules_Explanation)); *Bang!*, where eliminated players still win if their team wins ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bang!_(card_game))). **The pure-currency version has also been shipped as a deliberate substitute for agency:** Deceit's 5th Anniversary update — "Players that choose to spectate can now earn extra tickets as they watch based on what the remaining players do" — after players asked for spectator agency and the devs paid them in currency instead. Deceit 2 repeats it.

**Failure mode.** It fixes *motivation*, not *boredom*. It does nothing for the 90 seconds you are actually dead and staring at the ring — Deceit is the cautionary case of shipping only this. It is a layer, not an answer. Second: it is a rubber band and rubber bands need tuning — Crawl shipped "a more fair approach to wrath distribution" on Switch specifically to help players who were behind ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/switch-eshop/crawl)), and also "revamped the store teleporters, to give new heroes a better chance to go to the store for upgrades" ([Powerhoof devlog](https://www.powerhoof.com/tag/crawl/page/2/)).

**Godot cost: CHEAP.** Pure state, a HUD meter, a between-round spend screen. No netcode risk, no new entity types. **This is the layer to ship regardless of which other option wins** — it composes with all four.

**A headless bot match can measure.** This is the option a sim is *genuinely good at.* Whether the Wrath curve converges match scores or overcorrects; whether the leader's win-rate is still above chance after N rounds; whether the currency is ever left unspent (unspent resource = UI failure); variance of final match score across seeds.

**Kingmaking exposure: zero.** There is nothing to aim.

### #4 — THE DECOY: the dead prisoner as a tax on the shooter's attention

**How it works.** The shooter's scarce resources are *attention and ammunition*, not health. A shot prisoner can, on a strict meter, manifest briefly as a false silhouette on the ring — reads as a runner at a glance, resolves as a ghost on inspection. It cannot be killed for score and cannot block a shot; it costs the shooter a look, possibly a round and a reload. Non-lethal, non-blocking, aimed at the tower. Governed by **when-not-where** (you choose the moment; the manifestation point is fixed — where you died, or a fixed rail), a rate limit, and a shared pool across all ghosts so it cannot scale with the dead count.

**Proven in.** Only in pieces — treat this as **highest-upside, lowest-evidence.** **Bomberman's Revenge Cart** is the closest shipped shape (position on a rail, so it cannot choose whom to help). **TowerFall's Trigger Corpses** supply the timing primitive. **White Noise 2** is the best-liked non-lethal ghost in the corpus and the only one players spontaneously praise: dead players "Rise as a ghost and take advantage of your enhanced exploration skills to help the remaining investigators" ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/503350/White_Noise_2/)), with shout, cooldown-gated compass and flare, a dropped light that also *slows the monster*, banishing the creature's idols and disabling its decoys and traps — every verb navigation, illumination or de-buffing the monster's furniture, none of them lethal. **Unfortunate Spacemen** ghosts flicker lights on a 15s cooldown and whisper to the living ([v0.2.9 notes](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4249665521683298279)). **R.E.P.O.'s** possessed head is the tightest formulation of all — "Added battery that recharges while dead! Spend your battery charge to possess your head and interact with the living" ([V0.3.0](https://repo-2025horror.fandom.com/wiki/V0.3.0)) — zero lethality hard-coded, and the upgrade extends *duration* but never *recharge rate*, so the ceiling on continuous influence is fixed.

**Failure mode.** It cheapens the shooter's core skill. Target identification *is* the shooter's fantasy in a Duck Hunt-derived game; corrupting it deliberately may read as "the game is lying to me" rather than "my opponent outplayed me." Second: it scales with the dead count unless pooled (Law 1) — at 1v7 the late ring becomes a hall of mirrors. Third, subtler: *where* you manifest is a location choice, and on a ring, location is a runner — so it is not as kingmaking-clean as it first looks, which is exactly why when-not-where matters here. Fourth, from Phasmophobia: **audit every shared sensor.** Their observers cannot make sound when they throw things — a deliberate leash — but they *do* trigger motion sensors, so a pure-flavour ability became an information-pollution vector through a channel nobody was watching.

**Godot cost: MEDIUM.** A new ephemeral networked entity, a manifestation meter, and a "reads as a runner at distance, obviously ghostly up close" art/shader problem that is more art cost than code cost, plus rate limiting.

**A headless bot match can measure.** Shots-per-elimination and time-to-elimination with decoys on vs off. Wasted-shot rate. Whether decoy placement correlates with one runner's survival more than the field's. Where the scaling breaks (1 dead vs 6 dead).

### #5 — THE SPOTTER, DECAYING: fixed sensors and a rationed ping

**How it works.** The shot prisoner gets vision — but only from **pre-placed, fixed cameras** around the ring, never a free-flying camera of their own choosing — plus a hard-rationed ping. The ping's value decays: full strength for the first seconds after death, then throttled to nothing.

**Proven in.** The best-evidenced boundary in the survey, reached independently by two studios. Competitive Counter-Strike does **not** mute the dead — `sv_deadtalk 1` ships in tournament configs ([get5 `live.cfg`](https://github.com/splewis/get5/blob/master/cfg/get5/live.cfg)); what it restricts is the **camera**, via `mp_forcecamera 1`. Rainbow Six Siege has let dead operators read **fixed** map CCTV and bulletproof cams since 2015, uncontroversially; its 2021 proposal to let dead attackers **drive their own drones** was pulled from the Test Server within days ("Gameplay After Death will be removed from the Test Server tomorrow as we look at feedback" — [r/Rainbow6TTS](https://www.reddit.com/r/Rainbow6TTS/comments/nn5520/we_want_your_feedback_gameplay_after_death/), [Player.One](https://www.player.one/rainbow-six-siege-test-server-removed-gameplay-after-death-140524)) and never shipped. **The line both drew is not *alive vs dead* and not *voice vs silence* — it is whether the dead player can generate NEW information from a position they choose.** For the decay curve: VALORANT's Clutch Mute, Patch 4.11 (7 June 2022), deliberately a **manual toggle** so the clutching player still gets the one useful callout and *then* silences the coaching — "Tired of all the voices in your head when you're trying to clutch that 1v4?" ([Riot](https://playvalorant.com/en-us/news/game-updates/valorant-patch-notes-4-11/)). Ubisoft's stated goal is quotable in your design doc: "we want players to feel invested in their team's success, right up until the last second of the round."

**Failure mode. This one attacks the premise directly.** PANOPTICON rests on the shooter having a hard information advantage from the tower. Every ping erodes exactly that. It is also the most **kingmaking-exposed** option here — choosing whose lane to call out is choosing who survives — and the known vector for backseat toxicity (Apex's five-year-old unresolved "let me mute my dead teammate" thread was closed with no fix: [EA Forums](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/apex-legends-feedback-en/mute-teammates-after-you-die/8574498)). And you cannot suppress it socially: friends will use Discord regardless, so in-game rules only bind strangers — §0.3's argument, pointing the other way.

**Godot cost: CHEAP to build, EXPENSIVE to balance.** A camera and a ping marker are an afternoon. The information rules are the entire cost, and only a playtest settles them.

**Ranked last** not because it is bad, but because it is the one option whose *success case* damages the thing that makes PANOPTICON PANOPTICON.

### Explicitly rejected, and why

| Option | Why it is a poor fit here |
|---|---|
| **Shot prisoner becomes a second shooter** | Ruled out by the author — but note it is the cheapest historically-proven answer, and it is probably what PANOPTICON's own ancestor does. Halo's Infection converts rather than eliminates, so no dead-agency state exists ([Halopedia](https://www.halopedia.org/Infection)); the "Duck Duck Hunt" variant does this explicitly. See §5 on Duck Hunt's "backwards" construction. Everything above has to beat it on *feel*, not on cost. |
| **Betting / wagering on survivors** | Needs a round long enough for odds to move and a social table to read; a 2–6 minute FPS round gives neither. It is kingmaking bait by design — a bet gives the dead player a reason to want a *specific* runner to win. Any transferable currency inherits the skin-gambling and match-fixing problem. Darwin Project is the shipped experiment: an 11th "Show Director" player plus Twitch viewers voting on who receives nukes and heals ([Twitch blog](https://blog.twitch.tv/en/2018/07/24/scavengers-spectator-interactions-extension-makes-all-viewers-players-4a1b2ceb10f8/)); creative director Simon Darveau's own caveat is the lesson — "the whole game needs to be designed from the ground up around it" — and the game shut down. **Audience agency is a multiplier on a game that already works, not a substitute.** |
| **Commander / drafting AI / directing** | Wrong power fantasy — the loser gets promoted. Expensive. Adds AI the shooter must fight, diluting "one human shooter vs. human runners." Natural Selection 2's known dynamic is that a bad commander loses the game for everyone: kingmaking imported wholesale. |
| **Full body-possession of a living runner** | The most expensive thing to build in Godot netcode (authority churn, prediction, animation ownership), and it converts one player's boredom into another player's boredom. |
| **Plain respawn on a timer** | Trivialises the shooter's only verb. Any return must cost something the shooter can see (#2). |
| **Free-flying spectator camera as the whole answer** | Crab Game is the negative control — "Can now freecam spectate when eliminated" and nothing else in its entire update history; the dead are a *moderation* category ("Fixed an issue where you could not kick / ban dead players"), not a play category, and players describe it exactly: "**Instead of flying around and having nothing to do when you're spectating**…" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1782210/discussions/0/3191363817361395575/)). Unfortunate Spacemen's Zag rejected free-cam on a different and interesting ground — **atmosphere**: "I feel very strongly that a free-flying spectator camera would negatively affect the mood and atmosphere of the game." |

## 3. EVIDENCE — CRAWL IN DEPTH

**The game.** Powerhoof (Dave Lloyd + Barney Cumming, Melbourne, two people). A few-day local-multiplayer game-jam prototype became the studio's first project. Early Access 2014-08-06 → 1.0 2017-04-11 (PC/Mac/Linux/PS4/XB1) → Switch 2017-12-19. Up to 4 local players; bots fill empty seats at three difficulties. **~30-minute matches.** Metacritic 76 (XB1) / 82 (Switch). Over 10,000 copies in its first 12 days of Early Access. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crawl_(video_game)), [Powerhoof press sheet](https://www.powerhoof.com/press/sheet.php?p=crawl), [Powerhoof](https://www.powerhoof.com/crawl/))

**The dead-player loop, mechanically.**
- One player is the **Hero**; the others are **ghosts** — transparent, non-colliding with walls or objects, unable to touch the living, and limited only by **the edge of the screen**, i.e. leashed to the hero's room ([Crawl Wiki: Ghosts](https://crawl.fandom.com/wiki/Ghosts)).
- Ghosts damage the hero by **placing slimes** or by **possessing** furniture, traps, pentagrams, monster statues, slimes, boss parts and some chests.
- **Possession is a commitment**: once a spirit possesses a spawner or trap in the active room it "cannot leave the form until they die or kill the main character" ([Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/dungeon-game-friends-control-monsters/)). This is the anti-flicker-spam governor.
- **Monsters spawn from pentagrams**, and the spawn "rapidly cycle[s] through their available monsters before one is randomly selected" — deliberate randomness in the ghost's own toolset.
- Each player picks a **Deity** at match start, determining their monster roster.
- **Two parallel XP tracks**: hero XP unlocks attacks and spells; spirit XP unlocks stronger monsters to possess.
- **Wrath** is the catch-up currency, earned by ghosts **when the opposing Hero levels up**; evolutions cost 10 / 25 / 40, 75 total per line ([Crawl Wiki: Monsters](https://crawl.fandom.com/wiki/Monsters), [Monster Evolution Paths](https://crawl.fandom.com/wiki/Monster_Evolution_Paths)).
- **The killing blow converts you.** Whoever lands it becomes the Hero; the slain Hero becomes a ghost. **Your own hero level, weapons and stats are preserved between your turns** — you come back as *your* hero, not the dead one.
- **Dead time has an activity.** While the hero shops, ghosts fly around collecting coins and cash them for weak ooze monsters, useful for stealing last hits ([Steam guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=396032773), [steamsolo mirror](https://steamsolo.com/guide/a-simple-guide-to-crawl-crawl/)).
- **Endgame.** The hero must reach **level 10**, then fights a boss whose limbs and parts are **controlled cooperatively by all the ghosts**. Killing the boss wins the whole game — exactly one winner. If nobody gets there, reporting indicates the highest-XP player wins ([Use a Potion](https://www.useapotion.com/2018/01/crawl-review/)) — a scoring fallback so the match always resolves.

**Why it feels good rather than like a consolation prize — the developers' own account.**
- Barney Cumming on the social engine: *"players are, very quickly, changing up roles. One time you'll be teaming up with other monsters… but, when the Hero's almost dead, you'll start competing with each other."* And: *"That social dynamic of forming alliances and then switching them around and betraying each other. That's more fun than anything we could build in the actual game."* ([Power Up Gaming](https://powerupgaming.co.uk/2015/11/19/developer-interview-dungeon-crawling-with-powerhoof/))
- On the boss inversion: *"the ghosts can jump into different body parts and whoever kills the Boss wins the game that, again, turns the 'people dynamics' on its head. Because, when you're all controlling the Boss together, you're one hundred percent co-operative with the other ghosts – if the Hero wins, you all lose."*
- On the cost: *"Balance really is the hardest part of development on Crawl — to make sure it isn't frustrating for the hero or the monsters."* ([Cheerful Ghost](https://cheerfulghost.com/jdodson/posts/1993/interview-with-powerhoof-games-the-creators-of-crawl))
- Kotaku's assessment is the one-line version of this whole brief: **"at no moment is anyone waiting around for something to happen."**

**What Crawl's critics flag — failure modes to inherit.**
- **Chaos** at three simultaneous monster players ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/games/crawl/b/switch/archive/2018/01/11/teach-your-friends-to-kill-each-other.aspx)).
- **Last-hit theft** — "heartbreaking… when one of your monster brethren steal the killing blow from you." Ghost cooperation is unstable by design, which is the fun *and* the friction.
- **Stalling** — reviews name "opportunities for players to hold up the game."
- **Rubber-band tuning** — the Switch release shipped "a more fair approach to wrath distribution" ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/switch-eshop/crawl)).
- **Solo play is the weak case** — reviews are consistently softer on bot matches than on local multiplayer.

**The transferable lesson.** Copy the *structure* (§0.1), not the possession. Possession suits a top-down 2D dungeon; a first-person ring course wants a different verb.

## 4. EVIDENCE — THE FULL SURVEY

### 4.1 Convert instead of eliminate
**Halo Infection** — "When a survivor dies, they switch to the zombie team." Elimination is a **team transfer**, so match population is conserved. **Alpha Zombies** get distinguishing traits; **Last Man Standing** gets unique traits, "typically… a waypoint revealing their location to zombies," which is an anti-stall governor aimed at the *living*. Reach closed the escape hatch (suicide now infects you) and forcibly converts a survivor if a zombie quits — the population invariant is maintained by force ([Halopedia](https://www.halopedia.org/Infection)).
**Zombie Panic! Source** (2007) — "when you die, you become a zombie." Survival Mode gives the zombie team a shared **life pool** where "A Zombie life is gained if a human is killed" — the human's death *feeds* the enemy respawn pool, so elimination is a resource transfer rather than a subtraction; Objective Mode gives infinite zombie lives so the dead "can always join the action again" ([Combine OverWiki](https://combineoverwiki.net/wiki/Zombie_Panic!_Source)).
**Left 4 Dead Versus** — dead Survivors do not respawn; the Infected team does. Killed Infected enter **ghost mode**: invisible to Survivors, invulnerable, able to pass through doors and physics objects, and **unable to interact with anything** — a *pre-spawn state with zero action verbs*. Spawn timer scales with team size (~20–30s at four Infected, 5s solo); spawn placement is gated by distance and **Survivor line of sight**, so scouting cannot be converted into an ambush from nowhere; and a ghost that falls into an instant-kill zone is killed without spawning, **restarting the timer** — a cost on reckless ghost-scouting. Voice is firewalled: "You can't be heard in ghost mode." ([L4D wiki: Versus](https://left4dead.fandom.com/wiki/Versus)) Valve intended Versus as the only mode but "found it was very difficult to balance the game so that both teams could have fun."
**Depth** also ships a conversion mode — Hide and Seek (2015): "When a diver is killed they join the shark side."
*Works: reliably. Failure: snowball. And it is the option Ryan has ruled out for the canonical mode.*

### 4.2 Constrained hazard — the eliminated player acts, with a governor
**Bomberman's Revenge Cart / Bad Bomber** — the reference implementation, with four stacked governors: a **positional rail** (locked outside the Outer Wall Blocks), **verb reuse** (the only action is the game's existing verb, throw a bomb — no new systems for the living to track), a **re-entry ramp** (a kill puts you back in), and a **population floor** (community-sourced: it requires ≥2 players remaining, so it cannot decide a 1v1). Zero information asymmetry — the cart sees the same arena everyone sees. ([Konami R2 manual](https://www.konami.com/games/bomberman/r2/manual/en/switch/page08.html), [R Online manual](https://dds.konami.com/games/manual/sbro/multi/en/pc/page04.html), [SuperCheats on the trigger condition](https://www.supercheats.com/ps4/super-bomberman-r/1796/revenge-cart/), [openretro on SB3 1995](https://openretro.org/snes/super-bomberman-3), [IGN on Bomberman Ultra's Super Revenge Mode](https://www.ign.com/wikis/ps3-cheats/Bomberman_Ultra_Cheats))
**Crawl** — §3.
**TowerFall Ascension** — three opt-in Versus variants ([Variants](https://towerfall.wiki.gg/wiki/Variants)): *Return as Ghosts* (float freely, kill by touch, short dash attack — **and ghosts are killable by arrows, explosions or a head-stomp, so counterplay exists**); *Exploding Corpses* (posthumous scoring — your body is the weapon but you don't aim it); *Trigger Corpses* (hold Shoot/Jump/Dodge "for a few moments" to detonate — **you choose the WHEN, never the WHERE**). Documented mechanic-on-mechanic failure: stacking Ghosts with Exploding Corpses means "the ghosts usually now get instantly vaporized by the death explosion" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/251470/discussions/0/1474221865182562066/)). Note Quest/co-op has no ghost at all — respawn at your corpse with an AoE burst, and a partner can donate lives ([Quest](https://towerfall.wiki.gg/wiki/Quest)).
**Party Animals** — eliminated players get a **Spectator Menu**: "Spectators use coins to throw stuff at the remaining players. One coin is regenerated every twenty seconds. Spectators start with three coins and can hold a maximum of five," banana peel 2 / projectile 3 / bomb 4 ([wiki](https://partyanimals.wiki.gg/wiki/Spectator_Menu)). The lobby owner can disable throwing entirely. **The economy throttles the RATE of griefing but not the REASON — which is why it also needed the toggle**, the direct contrast with Bomberman, where re-entry removes the motive. Patched along the way: "Fixed an issue where Bomb No.2 will block the view of eliminated players" — the spectator's own hazard obstructing spectating. Downtime complaints persist regardless ([r/PartyAnimalsGame](https://www.reddit.com/r/PartyAnimalsGame/comments/172ndjb/)).
**Trouble in Terrorist Town** (2009) — dead spectators press USE on any physics prop to possess and punch it. Convars: `ttt_spec_prop_control` (default 1), `ttt_spec_prop_base` 8 punches, `ttt_spec_prop_force` 110 (nudge-scale, not lethal-scale), `ttt_spec_prop_rechargetime` 1s per point, and `ttt_spec_prop_maxbonus 16` / `maxpenalty -6` — **the meter cap scales with karma, so griefers are throttled automatically without a moderator.** Also `ttt_postround_dm` (a free-for-all sandbox for the dead *outside* the scored round) and `ttt_limit_spectator_chat` (in Sherlock mode the dead can only "mumble"). ([settings](https://www.troubleinterroristtown.com/config/settings/), [mechanics](https://troubleinterroristtown.wiki.gg/wiki/Game_mechanics)) The wiki names the failure mode itself — propelling objects at the living or **detonating explosive barrels** is "frowned upon or even outright banned" by server rules. **The tell: it ships enabled by default and is disabled *socially*, not technically.**
**Ultimate Chicken Horse** — nobody is ever out; a player who dies in a round **still places a block next round**, and the customisable rule set has no elimination rule at all. Death is scored in both directions: "Trap points are awarded every time another player dies on a trap you placed" and "Postmortem points are given to players who reach the goal after dying" ([dev docs](https://cleverendeavourgames.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/32000028991-custom-rules-and-presets), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_Chicken_Horse)). **And the dev reversal is the strongest single quote in this family** — from their 2026 sequel: "**During playtests, we found the game felt way too punishing if a single trap knocked you out for the entire round**," so respawn is now default and lethality is an opt-in Hardcore toggle ([Clever Endeavour](https://cleverendeavourgames.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/32000036282-why-do-players-respawn-after-hitting-traps-how-do-i-change-that-)). **Even ~30 seconds of dead time tested badly.** Note also that UCH has live kingmaking through trap points — you profit from whoever dies on your block, so late-game placement becomes targeted.
**Last Year: The Nightmare** — elimination abolished via an escalating respawn tax: "Classmate respawn times now increase by 15 seconds with each kill," plus respawn invulnerability that cancels on action. Both versions are now delisted — cautionary, not validated.
**Move or Die** — the pure control case for "shorten the bench": ~20-second rounds, everyone alive at the top of each one, and the design never needed a dead-player mechanic ([FAQ](https://www.moveordiegame.com/faq)).
**SpeedRunners — negative result, worth recording because the hypothesis is intuitive and wrong.** There is no laser, hazard, turret or sabotage for the eliminated player; they "simply sit out the remainder of the round." The only consequence of an elimination is that **the screen starts shrinking**, i.e. the pressure goes on the living. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpeedRunners), [Controls](https://speedrunners.wiki.gg/wiki/Controls)) **This is an accelerate-the-round design, not a staff-the-bench design.**
*Works: highest engagement of any family. Failure: every shipped instance needed a governor, several needed a toggle on top, and Crawl adds stalling.*

### 4.3 Support / environment shaping without lethality
**White Noise 2** — the only implementation players spontaneously praise. Store page leads with it: "**Back from the grave.** In White Noise 2, death doesn't mean game over. Rise as a ghost…" ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/503350/White_Noise_2/)). Ghost kit: shout (rendered in a distinct colour, extended sight range), cooldown-gated compass and flare, a dropped light that illuminates *and slows the monster*, banishing the creature's idols, disabling its decoys and Rusalka's traps — and the **budget-conservation governor**, "Ghosts can now break a single sigil, **if they didn't break one before dying**." Information is deliberately clipped: "Ghosts can no longer see the Scarecrows." A living perk keys off ghost play (Shaira's *Medium*: +10% ghost speed and interaction speed), so ghost play is build-relevant. Unprompted player quotes from Steam reviews: "when you are killed you dont wait watching other players till the game finish but you play as a ghost"; "you're now a ghost that helps your friends find clues easier, confounds the monster by destroying his decoys and sensor totems." **Failure mode: it is a one-sided rubber band** — ghost help is purely pro-survivor, so the monster's reward for a kill is a new opponent with different powers; devs nerfed ghost walk speed and idol-banish time within days of launch.
**Unfortunate Spacemen** — Geoff "Zag" Keene's rationale is the best in the corpus: the previous system "removed the dead players from enjoying the remainder of the match in any significant way," and "**I feel very strongly that a free-flying spectator camera would negatively affect the mood and atmosphere of the game**, so I have devised a solution that I feel is both unique and far more satisfying, interesting, and engaging than sitting around looking at cameras and clouds." Dead players become invisible ghosts on the map who "haunt the living by flickering lights and whispering sweet, terrifying nothings into their ears," with a flashlight only they can see ([v0.2.9](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4249665521683298279)). Leashes: an explicit comms firewall (ghosts "can not communicate with any of the Spacemen they are spectating"), a 15s cooldown on the only world-affecting verb, capability revoked the moment it touched the round's outcome ("Ghosts can no longer re-seal samples that were unsealed by the Traitor"), and score-farming blocked. **The design tell: omniscience plus flavour agency, almost no causal power.**
**Project Winter** — the richest ghost kit ever shipped, and a five-year chronology of governor discovery. Teleport to any living player (1s cooldown); four AoE abilities (+40 warmth / +40 hunger / +40 health / **−30 warmth, i.e. a ghost can kill by cold**); one inventory slot (and "If ghosts pick up poison, they can use it to poison interactable objects"); ghost-specific bonus challenges; a Ghost Power resource with smoke bombs, flares, tripwires; **"Even in the afterlife, your voice matters! Dead players can now vote for the global events that living players experience"**; and **Wild Spirit** — "As a ghost, players can take revenge on their killer by transforming into the wild spirit of a bear!" Then the walk-back: Ghost Power added four years in; Wild Spirit nerfed four times in seven months (no power regen while transformed, 180s cooldown, **voice muted while a bear**, cannot transform in doorways because bears bodyblock entrances); a whole-feature kill switch; and finally, after a **1000-response community survey**, "We are removing both the Ally role and Wild Spirit (ghost bear) from Normal modes." **And it still draws "Being a ghost is too boring, and matches take too long."**
**R.E.P.O.** — "Added battery that recharges while dead! Spend your battery charge to possess your head and interact with the living" ([V0.3.0](https://repo-2025horror.fandom.com/wiki/V0.3.0), [Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/r-e-p-o-v0-3-0-monster-update-full-patch-notes-listed/)). Zero lethality hard-coded; the upgrade extends *duration* only, never recharge rate; the only two verbs are self-recovery and warning. Failure mode: over-tuned — players "spend more time waiting for it to recharge than actually using it." **Both failure modes on one dial.**
**Super Mario Bros. Wonder** — Ghost form and **Standees**. The Standee is the interesting inversion: a *living* player converts a consumable into permanent environment that services the dead ([Standee](https://www.mariowiki.com/Standee)). Contrast the NSMB Wii/U **Bubble** — near-zero but non-zero agency (shake the remote to drift toward teammates; you cannot self-pop) and the standard voluntary-bubbling parasite complaint.
**Among Us** — ghosts keep doing tasks (crew) or **sabotages** (impostor). The design logic is precise and transplantable: *kill and vote are aimed, so they are stripped at death; sabotage is map-wide and hits all living crew at once, so it is safe to leave in.* Dev rationale for ghost tasks, Marcus Bromander (1 Oct 2020): he had "heard from players who were simply leaving the match after death, because there wasn't much for them to do" ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/among-us-ghost-gameplay-change/)). The failure mode is a hostage problem — ghost tasks are *mandatory* for the task-win, so "if one ghost goes afk you automatically can't win via tasks." **And Innersloth reversed it in Hide n Seek: "Ghosts cannot do tasks. Instead, they are prompted to 'enjoy the chaos.'"** Also: any ghost action with a world-visible animation is an alibi-manufacturing machine — players report ghosts triggering visual-task animations that vouch for a living partner.
**Phasmophobia** — dead players become **observers** who phase through locked doors, sprint infinitely and are ignored by the ghost; they can manipulate non-investigation props but **"throwing items as an observer does not trigger a sound,"** and the highest-value evidence (D.O.T.S., shapeshifting, cold breath) is hidden *from* the dead ([Death](https://phasmophobia.fandom.com/wiki/Death)). Presence granted, every signal channel severed. **The one they forgot:** observers trigger motion sensors, which can mislead the living — a pure-flavour ability became an information-pollution vector because it touched a *shared sensor*. **Audit every shared sensor, not just the obvious comms channels.**
**Escape the Backrooms** ships three different answers as a difficulty axis, one patch line each: easy "only require one player to find the exit" (the dead are carried); normal "spectate players when you die"; hard "kills every player if one player dies" (no dead time because there is no dead player).
**Damned** — a negative case with a host kill switch: no ghost body, no haunting; the fix for dead time was to *reach spectator faster* ("Changed time to enter spectator mode when dying from 10 seconds to 5 seconds"), and spectating itself is a host-toggled server setting.
*Works: griefing- and kingmaking-safe when the effect is global and non-lethal. Failure: hollowness, one-sided rubber-banding, and — per Project Winter — boredom that content cannot fix.*

### 4.4 Fixed-sensor intel and the ghosting problem
Covered in shortlist #5. The short version: **restrict where the dead player can LOOK; leave the talking alone.** Competitive CS ships `sv_deadtalk 1` with `mp_forcecamera 1`; Siege shipped fixed cams in 2015 and killed driveable drones in 2021; VALORANT's Clutch Mute is the decay-curve answer. **A dead player's information is high-value for about three seconds and net-negative thereafter — design a decay curve, not a binary.** Apex is the counter-example: a dead player cannot open the map to ping, so their only channel is voice — the exact channel Respawn built the ping system to avoid, having playtested for a month with all mics muted "to eliminate opportunity for toxicity" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/respawn-played-with-muted-mics-to-get-i-apex-legends-i-smart-comms-system-just-right)). **Witch It** is the cleanest documented case of shipping spectator interaction and then revoking it: "As an attempt to prevent Ghosting we changed the visibility of witches for spectators" (0.5.1), then "Hunters can no longer see spectators" (1.1.2), then "Regular spectators can no longer toss potatoes in Imposturous" (1.3.5). *The revocation is documented; the stated reason is not — treat the ghosting explanation as strong circumstantial inference.* Esports rulebooks police *non-participants* rather than the dead: ESIC's ghosting definition is about stream-sniping, and the 2020 CS:GO coach spectator-bug scandal is the enforcement precedent ([ESIC](https://esic.gg/sanction-outcome/esports-integrity-commission-esic-statement-regarding-stream-sniping-ghosting-in-online-csgo-tournaments/)). The question competitive CS actually asks is "did you earn this information by being in the match?" — not "are you alive?"

### 4.5 Re-entry economies (agency lives with the living)
**Warzone's Gulag** — §2 #2. Note the failed variant: **Gulag 2.0** (Warzone 2.0, Nov 2022) made it 2v2 with a forced enemy alliance, proximity chat and an NPC "Jailer" — reverted to 1v1 in Season 2, with the Jailer replaced by **an overtime point-capture timer** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_of_Duty:_Warzone_2.0)). **Apex** banner → beacon; the **Mobile Respawn Beacon** is visible on every player's minimap, an explicit anti-abuse tax. **Fortnite Reboot Vans** — the negative control; teammate-gated revival converts the dead player's agency into someone else's chore, and with strangers the chore doesn't get done ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/the-good-and-bad-of-fortnites-new-respawn-system-1833951661)). **PUBG's Recall / Blue Chip** (Update 23.2, May 2023) — recall "demands cooperation and effort from surviving team members rather than just the fallen player"; disabled from Phase 7, max 6 per player ([official](https://pubg.com/en/news/6292)). Baseline PUBG dead-player agency is nil — no ping, no map marker. **PAYDAY 2 Police Custody** — the dead player is a hostage-trade token, and killing civilians adds 30–90s per civilian to the trade clock, so *the living team's misbehaviour extends the dead player's sentence* ([wiki](https://payday.fandom.com/wiki/Police_Custody)). **Left 4 Dead Rescue Closets** — 60 seconds, then you materialise if the team is at medium range, and you **vanish if the group moves away**; disabled in Realism. **Don't Starve Together** — the ghost is given not agency but **leverage**: it inflicts a stacking sanity drain on the living until they pay 40 HP to craft a Telltale Heart and revive it ([wiki](https://dontstarve.wiki.gg/wiki/Telltale_Heart)). **Friday the 13th's Tommy Jarvis** — one scarce, upgraded slot.
*Works: notably, in these designs the dead player has near-zero agency and nobody complains, because the drama is in the living team's budget. Failure: chore transfer, and dead-time the dead player cannot shorten.*

### 4.6 Give the eliminated player their own win condition
**Jackbox's Trivia Murder Party** is the cleanest instance in games. Wrong answers send you to the **Killing Floor**; losing there makes you a **ghost, not an eliminated player** — you keep answering every question, and the **Final Round triggers when only one player is alive**, at which point *everyone including all ghosts* races down a hallway and the first out wins ([Jackbox](https://www.jackboxgames.com/games/trivia-murder-party), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jackbox_Party_Pack_3)). The ghost's entire mid-game is banked into a real chance to win outright. **Blood on the Clocktower** — the dead keep talking, keep private conversations, keep exactly **one ghost vote**, and still win or lose with their team; designer Steven Medway: "When you die you have lost your power, but you've not lost your ability to work as a team," and the wiki concedes "the game is usually decided by the votes and opinions of the dead players." **Town of Us's Phantom and Haunter** are the video-game version *with counterplay* — see §1's eighth governor.
*Works: these are the most acclaimed designs in the survey. Failure: BotC's is textbook kingmaking, deliberately embraced and neutralised only because the dead remain bound to a win condition.*

### 4.7 Shorten the bench instead of solving it
**Sea of Thieves' Ferry of the Damned** — 15s solo up to 25s for a four-crew, deliberately scaled so a bigger crew's death costs less individually; on the Ferry you can walk, swap weapons, claim Flames of Fate and talk to other dead players **including dead enemies** — and because the Ferry is a separate space with proximity voice, **a dead pirate's voice can never reach their living crew: zero information transfer, by construction** ([wiki](https://seaofthieves.wiki.gg/wiki/Ferry_of_the_Damned)). **Move or Die** (20s rounds). **One Night Ultimate Werewolf** compresses Werewolf to a single night specifically so it "does not exclude players who are eliminated early" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_Werewolf)). The negative controls: **CoD Search & Destroy**, whose own official mode guide coaches players not to sprint to their death and "spectat[e] the rest of the round"; **Evolve**, where the dead Hunter waits for a dropship that "by default arrives every two minutes" plus ~30s of animation, and that wait is *explicitly the Monster's reward window* ([Prima guide](https://primagames.com/eguides/evolve-official-strategy-guide/hunter-training/reviving-teammates)) — the loudest player revolt in the corpus; **Killing Floor 2**, where you cannot respawn until the wave is over and the community server ecosystem re-invented mid-wave respawn as a *priced* resource (escalating delay, "Every respawn also deducts 20% of your current money," and respawning *increases wave difficulty*); **League of Legends**, where death timers deliberately grow to 78.75s max — the industry standard is that dead-player downtime is made *worst* exactly when the game is most exciting to sit out of ([LoL wiki](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/Death)).

## 5. THE KINGMAKING HAZARD

### 5.1 The formal definition, and why it is a targeting problem
The tightest formulation: kingmaking is when "a player that **cannot win the game themselves** ceases their attempt to win on their own behalf and instead puts their influence behind another chosen player to ensure a win for them" ([No Rerolls](https://norerolls.co.uk/2021/03/23/kingmaking-in-board-games/)). Dave Eng adds the load-bearing nuance — the player "can still wield their own power, agency, and influence" but "cannot do so in order to win": a **lame-duck** situation ([University XP](https://www.universityxp.com/blog/2021/7/6/what-is-kingmaking)). Lewis Pulsipher named it the **petty diplomacy problem**, one of four multiplayer anti-patterns alongside **turtling, leader bashing and sandbagging**.

**Richard Garfield's definition of the parent concept is the one to design against:** politics is "**the capability of one player to pick on another**" ([Opinionated Gamers](https://opinionatedgamers.com/2012/07/02/the-art-of-design-interviews-to-game-designers-20-richard-garfield/)). Mark Rosewater's version: "**If your opponents are able to suggest what you should do, it's political**," and he concludes the only real removal is house rules dictating exactly who each player must attack.

**Kingmaking is a consequence of targetability, not of elimination.** That reframing is the most useful thing in this section for PANOPTICON.

Two flavours needing different defences: **spite** (aimed at a person → remove targeting) and **spoiling** (an incidental strategy that hands someone the win → hide the game state). An orthogonal axis: **game-state-based** kingmaking (rooted in the board; broadly tolerated) vs **metagaming-based** (grudges, who won last week; universally hated).

Björk & Holopainen's pattern framework registers `Kingmaker` with a list of **facilitator patterns** — the prerequisites without which it cannot occur: *Dominant Strategies, Early Elimination, Early Leaving Players, Predictable Consequences, Predictable Winner, Possibility of Graceful Surrender, Unwinnable Game States.* That is a checklist of causes, not symptoms. A 2024 Uppsala thesis applies it to four board games and reaches a warning worth keeping: "**there is no one solution that fits all games, and a targeted rule modification is required on a game-by-game basis**" — they could not remove kingmaking from any of the four, only reduce its frequency ([PDF](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1876522/FULLTEXT01.pdf)).

### 5.2 The defences games actually ship

| Defence | Named exemplar | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Restrict who you can affect** | Magic's **Limited Range of Influence**, Comprehensive Rules §801: range "covers spells, abilities, effects, damage dealing, attacking, making choices, **and winning the game**"; §801.11 "it gets only information from within its controller's range of influence"; §801.14 scopes victory itself ([CR text](https://media.wizards.com/2025/downloads/MagicCompRules%2020250207.txt)). **Vampire: The Eternal Struggle** (Garfield, 1994) hard-codes it into seating — you may only oust your *prey*, the player on your left. | The strongest defence. Targeting, information and victory are the *same* primitive. |
| **Make the effect symmetric / non-targeted** | **King of Tokyo** — whoever is in Tokyo hits everyone else; everyone else hits only Tokyo; there is no "attack Bob" move. Garfield: "I set out to make it highly interactive but not super political and I think it was a success in that regard." **Among Us dead impostors keep sabotage** (map-wide) but lose kill and vote (aimed). | The most directly transplantable shape for PANOPTICON. |
| **Do not let a targeted effect be an accusation** | Among Us's Guardian Angel shield **works on Impostors too**, deliberately. Players demanded it not; the counter is that a role-filtered shield would "tell you who the impostor is" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/945360/discussions/0/4140564026495854123/)). **A shield that only lands on crewmates IS an accusation.** | Under-appreciated. Any dead-player ability whose *legal targets* are a subset of players leaks that subset. |
| **Never grant power and omniscience together** | Innersloth's rule, visible in the mechanics: a ghost that **Haunts** sees a player's role but "can no longer move on their own while Haunting"; a **Guardian Angel** gains a shield but **loses Haunt entirely** ([Guardian Angel](https://among-us.fandom.com/wiki/Guardian_Angel), [Haunt](https://among-us.fandom.com/wiki/Haunt)). | Ghosts trade mobility for omniscience; angels trade omniscience for power. Cheap and clean. |
| **Hide the score / hide who you'd be helping** | Puerto Rico's concealed victory track; CATAN's hidden development-card VPs. Pulsipher: "if a player does not know that they are losing they are less likely to kingmake." | Cheap in a video game, which *separates* information rather than presenting it equally. |
| **Give the dead their own win condition** | Trivia Murder Party's ghost-to-victory path; Crawl's killing blow; Bomberman's Revenge Cart return. | Converts kingmaking into competition: the dead cannot *pick* a winner, they must *steal* the win. |
| **Rate-limit influence** | Blood on the Clocktower's **single permanently-spent ghost vote**. | Influence as a scarce token, not a free action. |
| **Restrict information / lock the channel** | Mafia's "the dead do not speak," traceable to Davidoff's 1986 origin — a dead player holds hard private information and could hand the game to a faction with none of the risk a living player bears for saying the same thing ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mafia_(party_game))). | In a video game the *sensor* is the enforceable lever, not the channel (§4.4). |
| **Force the move** | Duncan's **Stop-Next Rule** and **McCarthy's Revenge Rule** — the kingmaker keeps their action but loses their discretion. | A genuinely distinct class: remove the *choice*, not the *power*. |
| **Legitimise it, then fence it** | Survivor / Big Brother juries: sequestration as an information firewall, the final-four fire-making challenge (S35, 2017) to shrink the social decision, Redemption Island / Edge of Extinction to drain the bitter-juror pool. | If you cannot remove it, stage it. |
| **Randomise or obscure the ending** | Unpredictable end-game triggers, so no move is legibly decisive. | Attacks the *Predictable Consequences* facilitator. |
| **Tournament rules** | Poker's **"one player to a hand"** — no player may advise or assist another's live hand, *including a busted player*. | The purest codification. |
| **Align the interests — the deepest fix** | If the eliminated player still shares a win condition with someone, kingmaking is structurally impossible. This is why dead-teammate comms are uncontroversial in CS and Valorant (same team) and called ghosting in a free-for-all. BotC: "you still win or lose when your team wins or loses." | If the dead player's *team* survives their elimination, you need none of the above. |

**Do not treat kingmaking as settled as a defect.** Cole Wehrle on *Root*: "it doesn't have a kingmaking problem, *it is* the kingmaking problem" ([Skeleton Code Machine](https://www.skeletoncodemachine.com/p/kingmaking)). Stewart Woods' *Eurogames* survey found roughly a third of respondents consider it a legitimate psychological skill. And one designer's reframe is worth carrying: kingmaking is not the disease, the **runaway leader** is — kingmaking is what irrelevance feels like from the inside.

### 5.3 PANOPTICON's specific exposure

The dead prisoner has exactly one rival class (the other prisoners, competing to finish) and one shared enemy (the shooter). So:

- **Actions aimed at the shooter are close to kingmaking-safe** — they help every living runner at once. The King of Tokyo / Among-Us-sabotage shape.
- **Actions aimed at a location are kingmaker-exposed in proportion to how spatially separated the runners are.** On a **ring course with runners strung out along it, a location-aimed action is effectively a runner-aimed action.** This is the crux, and it is easy to miss: **ring geometry silently converts "environmental" effects into targeted ones.**
- Ranked exposure of the shortlist: **#5 Spotter (highest — choosing whose lane to call out is choosing who survives) > #1 Haunt (high, via ring geometry) > #4 Decoy (medium, same reason) > #2 Return (low — self-interested by construction) > #3 Banked Run (zero — nothing to aim).**
- Defences available, roughly cheapest first: make the effect **whole-ring rather than local**; adopt **when-not-where** (TowerFall) so the ghost picks the moment, not the place; make the effect spend a **shared pool the living choose how to use**; **invert the ghost's information** so they see the shooter and *not* the other runners' positions (a direct application of Magic §801.11 — you cannot aim at what you cannot see); check that the ability's **legal target set does not itself leak** (the Guardian Angel lesson); make the effect **cost the ghost something in their own match score**; make the ghost's payoff **independent of which runner wins**; and, best of all, give the ghost a **re-entry ramp** so they are playing for themselves.

## 6. THE 1v1 EDGE CASE: no living runner at all

At 1v1, a shot prisoner means the round has nobody left to interact with. The industry has converged on an answer, independently, in at least three games:

**When the interaction partner disappears, do not leave the mechanic pointed at nothing — repoint it at the clock.**

- **Dead by Daylight** is the definitive case study, and it is precisely a "what happens when everyone else is dead" rule, re-patched for six years. Launch: the Hatch appeared once completed generators = alive survivors + 1, producing **untimed hatch standoffs** where killer and last survivor circled indefinitely. **Patch 2.7.0 (7 May 2019)** added the **Endgame Collapse**: "End Game Collapse starts when an exit gate is opened or when the Killer closes the hatch" — PTB tested 3 minutes, live shipped 2, slowed while a survivor is downed or hooked, survivors still present at expiry auto-sacrificed ([Patch 2.7.0](https://deadbydaylight.wiki.gg/wiki/Patch_2.7.0)). **Patch 5.3.0 (19 Oct 2021)** restricted the hatch to appear "only when one survivor is left alive," and the stated reason names the degenerate incentive outright: it "reduce[s] situations where survivors would camp the hatch, **waiting for their teammates to die** so they could escape" ([Patch 5.3.0](https://deadbydaylight.wiki.gg/wiki/Patch_5.3.0)). The invariant across every patch: **the last-player exit is a single-winner resource on a clock, never shared and never untimed.** In DbD's own 1v1 custom lobbies there is no special-casing beyond scaling the generator requirement — the "last survivor alone" state is simply *the only state*.
- **Bomberman** takes the blunt version of the same answer: the Revenge Cart requires **≥2 players remaining**, so it structurally cannot decide a 1v1 *(community-sourced)*.
- **Warzone 2.0** replaced the Jailer NPC with **an overtime point-capture system** in Season 2 — the same "round just ends" resolution.
- **Halo Infection** uses a **"Last Man Standing"** buff/debuff on the final human (typically a waypoint revealing them) to prevent a passive stall.
- **Warzone's** answer to a fully-wiped squad is the bluntest: no Gulag, no buyback, pure spectator, no special case written.
- **Among Us** simply forbids the count: a hard **4-player minimum** since launch. The math underneath is the Braverman–Etesami–Mossel result that mafia count must scale as **√P** to keep faction win probabilities balanced — at small P the minimum integer of 1 is already disproportionately large, so the round resolves by attrition before deduction can operate.
- **Hunt: Showdown** offers the other viable answer: a **formal solo-conditional rule class**. "Solo Traits" are traits that "gain a bonus conditional effect for players who go into a match without teammates" — Necromancer's self-revive, plus Conduit, Magpie and Serpent, introduced in Update 1.12 ([Traits](https://huntshowdown.wiki.gg/wiki/Traits)). "You are alone" becomes a first-class game state with its own rules rather than a degenerate edge case.
- **Evolve** used **bot backfill** to preserve the 4v1 shape at any headcount; **Crawl** does the same, filling empty seats with bots at three difficulty levels.

**What this means for PANOPTICON, concretely.** Three coherent positions:

1. **The clock.** At 1v1, the round ends the instant the runner is shot. Score on distance and time survived, rotate the tower immediately, next round. Gate the dead-player mechanic to **≥2 living runners at round start** — the Bomberman rule. This is the majority industry answer and it costs nothing to build. It also reframes the problem: at 1v1, the fix for sitting out is a **short round and an instant rotation**, not a ghost mechanic.
2. **A solo-conditional rule class**, Hunt-style: at 1v1 the shot prisoner gets a different, explicitly-authored ability set. Honest about the fact that 1v1 is a different game; the most expensive option.
3. **Choose a mechanic whose referent survives.** The sharpest observation in the brief: **options aimed at the shooter still have a target when every runner is dead; options aimed at helping runners do not.** #1 (Haunt) and #4 (Decoy) still function at 1v1 — a lone ghost can still harass the tower. #5 (Spotter) has literally nobody to talk to. #2 (Return) is arguably *better* at 1v1, because it is the only thing keeping the round alive.

And one caution the DbD history makes explicit: whatever rule you write for "last runner alive," check whether it creates an incentive for runners to **want** their teammates dead. BHVR had to patch exactly that.

The Halo: Reach Duck Hunt gametype files could not be retrieved this session (GameFAQs and HaloCustoms are captcha- and 403-walled to automated fetch). What Halopedia does say is suggestive: **"Duck Hunt had to be built 'backwards,' with the hunter implemented as a lone survivor and the prey as zombies"** ([Infection](https://www.halopedia.org/Infection)).

Worth doing before committing to a design.

## 8. WHAT ONLY A PLAYTEST CAN SETTLE

A headless bot match can measure effect size, win-rate deltas, round length, exploit EV, and — usefully — kingmaking exposure as a Δ P(win) spread across living runners. It cannot settle any of the following, and none of them should be argued about in the abstract:

1. **Whether it reads as a role or as a consolation prize.** This is the entire brief and it is unmeasurable. Crawl passes because being a ghost is a *seat*, not a penalty box. A sim cannot tell you whether your ghost feels like a monster or like a menu.
2. **Whether the ghost understands what their button did.** Legibility is the difference between "I cut the lights and he lost the shot" and "something flickered." An illegible effect is worse than no effect — it teaches the dead player that their agency is fake.
3. **Whether the shooter experiences interference as opposition or as cheating.** The shooter's fantasy is precision target ID from a position of advantage. A sim will tell you the decoy cost them 1.4 shots per elimination; only a human will tell you whether that felt like being outplayed or being lied to.
4. **Where the dead-player value decay curve actually sits.** VALORANT's evidence says roughly three seconds of high value, then net-negative. Whether that is 3s or 20s in a 4-minute ring course is a playtest question.
5. **What the ghosts say to each other, and whether it is fun or toxic.** Every dead-player mechanic is also a social channel. Crawl's developers say the shifting alliances are "more fun than anything we could build in the actual game" — that only shows up with humans in a room.
6. **Whether the ring-geometry problem is real in practice.** The location-equals-runner argument in §5.3 is a strong theoretical prediction. Runners may bunch enough, or the course may loop tightly enough, that it never bites.
7. **The chaos ceiling.** At what ghost count does the ring stop being readable? Crawl hits it at three. PANOPTICON's is a first-person 3D view down a scope, which is likely *lower*.
8. **Where the one dial sits.** R.E.P.O.'s law says you cannot tune out both griefing and ghost boredom; you can only choose. Only players can tell you which side of that they would rather live on.
9. **Whether the return (#2) makes the elimination feel worthless.** Ubisoft's "strictly better to stay alive" rule is checkable in a sim as an EV question; whether the *shooter* still feels rewarded is not.

**Three things the corpus says to ship alongside whatever you pick**, all cheap:
- **A toggle.** Every studio that shipped real dead-player power also shipped a way to turn it off. The absence of one is why Siege's version was killed outright rather than made optional.
- **A round-length target.** Content does not cure ghost boredom; round length does. Project Winter shipped six ghost systems and still drew "being a ghost is too boring, *and matches take too long*."
- **A shared ghost pool**, so the mechanic does not scale with the dead count. This is the one law that PANOPTICON's 1v7 upper bound makes non-negotiable.
