## Part 3. Synthesis

### What the strong ones (Nova, Caleb, RujiK, Codeer) share that the weak ones (O and Co, Squidge, Dani-at-launch) lack

1. A claim or a situation in the first ten seconds, not a studio intro. Nova: "I'm breaking the number one rule." Caleb: "17, dream game, deep end." RujiK: an absurd one-line premise with the game already on screen. Codeer: one line of premise, then straight into things he built. O and Co opens with "we are a humble and small indie studio"; Dani with "welcome to my very first devlog"; Squidge's confession is good but then spends 40 s on taste before the game. The title carries the claim too: "Breaking the No.1 Rule", "At 17 Years Old". "Introduction", "Devlog #0", "(The Right Way)" carry nothing.

2. Seconds to first own gameplay is NOT the discriminator. RujiK 0 s, Codeer ~0 s, Dani ~0 s, Squidge ~0 s, O and Co ~32 s, Caleb ~6:27, Nova ~10:05. The two biggest relative outperformers had the least gameplay early. What they had instead was a reason to keep watching (a bet, a person with something to lose). For a no-face devlog this means the voice has to carry a situation, not a "hey everyone".

3. Length. Strong: 13:26, 10:32, 7:27, 5:09. Weak: 4:17, 6:29, 6:03. The two longest did best relatively. A 10-13 min target is supported, provided it has an arc.

4. A real failure beat, told as a story not an aside. Nova spends ~110 s in tutorial hell with a named bug. Caleb admits three (palette scrapped, tilemap nightmare, rule tiles shelved). RujiK: snake-not-dinosaur, freaky knees, broken water. Codeer: "a worse Risk of Rain 2", the robot that does not walk flawlessly. Dani and Squidge have them too (Frankenstein stickman; "at this rate I'd never finish"). O and Co has none and is the most promotional; its series decayed fastest.

5. Game explained in one plain sentence, placed either in the first line (RujiK, Codeer, Dani, Squidge) or after the personal stakes (Nova 4:19, Caleb 1:58). Both placements worked. What never happens in the good ones is a feature list. The best single premise statement is Squidge's: genre, three inspirations, setting, one objective, in 30 seconds.

6. Explicit personal stakes. Nova: unemployed, on savings. Caleb: 17, dead channel, deep end. Squidge: ten years, never shown anyone. RujiK: zero subscribers as a joke. O and Co: "we'd like to get paid" (abstract). Stakes make the "next video" promise matter.

7. One technical explainer at layman level, with a picture. RujiK's foot-step rule, Caleb's random walker, Codeer's leg rebalance. These are retention devices in the middle third.

8. CTA at the very end only, 15-90 s, and it does not seem to matter how heavy it is (Nova's three-part ask did fine; Codeer had none and did fine). None of them ask early.

9. Deadpan beats a hype voice. RujiK, Codeer, Dani, Nova all under-sell with a cutaway to the thing failing. Nobody who did well said "epic" or "work of art".

10. A specific "next": Nova "world generation", Caleb "enemy AI", Squidge "enemies", Codeer (none, and he's the exception). Say what the next log will contain.

11. Devlog 0 still contains built things. Codeer's "0" had three finished features; Nova's had a controller, inventory and breaking; RujiK's one creature. Caleb's was mostly planning and still did well, but on the strength of the title.

### What to copy for a no-face voiceover
- The voice is the person. Nova and Caleb have no "who I am" beyond a situation and a motive; that's enough. Do not read a bio.
- Match every sentence to a visible state change (RujiK). If a sentence has no footage, cut it or put a diagram on screen.
- Leave music-only gaps for the work (Codeer: three 30-40 s montages in a 7-minute video).

## Recommended outline for PANOPTICON devlog 0 (a development log, not a trailer)

Target: 11-13 minutes, ~2,400 words at the measured ~190 wpm plus three footage-only gaps (~60 s total). At a slower 150 wpm the same words run ~16 min; trim to ~2,000 if the read is slow.

| Time | Section | Words | What it does | Footage needed from the game |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:45 | Hook | 130 | A claim or situation in the first sentence (three options below). End on a sting, not a title card. | The sniper's scope view from the tower; a runner lapping the lane; one clean kill; cut to black. 10-15 s total, real captures. |
| 0:45-1:30 | What the game is | 150 | One plain sentence: one sniper on a tower, everyone else runs laps, N64 look, for friends. Then the four maps in four cuts, one line each. No feature list. | Four map flyovers (EEVEE renders are fine for the flyovers; gameplay for the lane). |
| 1:30-3:00 | Why this game, why for friends | 250 | The bet. Why a novelty FPS and not a "real" one; why multiplayer for a group of mates instead of a store audience; why N64 look (speed, performance, one person can finish it - Squidge's line). This is the stakes beat. | B-roll of the editor, the map on a grid, a friend's voice clip if there is one. |
| 3:00-4:30 | The core loop as it stands | 280 | Walk through one round: what the sniper sees, what a runner does, what makes a lap tense, how a round ends. Deadpan; let the footage carry it. | One full round captured from both seats, split or intercut. Scoreboard. |
| 4:30-6:30 | The work: tower, lane, four maps | 400 | The actual log. Design the whole place first, then the scale chain, then the silhouettes; why each map is shaped the way it is. ### Three hook options drawn from what worked

1. Contrarian claim (Nova's structure). First line states the rule being broken: everyone says do not make a multiplayer FPS alone, or do not make a game for twelve people. Then the one-line premise, then "it can't be that hard, right?" and a cut to it being hard. Title would carry the same claim.

2. Confession plus premise (Squidge/Caleb). First line is the situation: how long it has been built, how few people have played it, and that those people are the point. Then the premise in one sentence. Stakes come free.

3. Deadpan premise with footage from second zero (RujiK/Codeer). The game is on screen before a word; first line is the whole premise, flat: one of you is on a tower, the rest of you run. Then "here is what that looks like" and a 10-second round. Lowest-risk, suits a no-face format, relies on the footage being good.

Whichever is chosen: hook complete by 0:40, game sentence by 1:00 at the latest, first failure by 6:30, CTA only in the last minute, specific "next" named.
