# Example Short Descriptions

Source: Chris Zukowski, "How To Market A Game" course. Lecture audio 7:22 (speech ends ~7:16, the rest is outro). Transcribed locally with whisper large-v3 (mlx-whisper); light cleanup, filler removed.

Hi, welcome to the next lecture, Example Short Descriptions. Writing short descriptions is very hard, especially if you've never written one before. I think it helps a lot to look at some examples that I've pulled from Steam. I'm going to show you some that are really great, and I'm going to show you some that need a little work. But please go soft on these. Even the ones that I say need a little work, there's a really complicated process that goes behind writing them. We don't know the intent behind the author. It could be that there were some political decisions why the short descriptions got made the way they are, or it could be that they had a bigger goal that they were trying to achieve with those short descriptions. It's not to say that these are bad. It's just to say that, in the context of what my opinion is on short descriptions, I think they need a little change. So please don't email these devs and tell them their short descriptions suck or something. They don't. It's just not the type of short description that I like to write. Let me show you what I mean.

This is the first one. It comes from the game Phantom Doctrine. I'm just going to read it to you: "The year is 1983. The world teeters on the verge of destruction as the Cold War tightens its icy grip. East or West, trust no one, question everything. The fate of humanity rests in your hands as you pit spy versus spy in tactical Phantom Doctrine."

Here are the problems that I have with this short description. It's a little bit too heavy on narrative. We get a lot of the context that they're trying to evoke with the short description from the screenshots, so we don't need to over-explain things that are better served by images. The other thing I don't really like is that it's impossible to tell what type of game it is. There's no clear genre other than the word "tactical". And you don't really know what you do in the game. If I pull out just the verbs: teeters, tightens, trust, question, rests. Those are the verbs that are in the short description. But this is not a game about teetering or tightening. So there are no verbs that actually describe what you do in the game. If you didn't know anything about this game, there is very little to tell you whether this is a first-person shooter, a strategy game; this could even be a visual novel. You need to be very clear on your genre when you're writing your short description.

Let's go to another example of a short description that I think needs a little bit of work. It's from the game Empire of Sin: "Empire of Sin is a new strategy game brought to you by Romero Games and Paradox Interactive that puts you at the heart of the ruthless criminal underworld of 1920s Prohibition-era Chicago."

There are a bunch of great things I like about this. For example, there's social proof, because they've mentioned Romero Games and Paradox, really world-renowned developers, being put right up front. That's pretty good. I like that. The bad thing is the word "strategy". That's a little too broad. Is it a real-time strategy game, a military strategy game, a 4X turn-based? Is it a builder game? Strategy is very broad, and there's a lot of subdivision that you need to get into to help describe this game to players who are shopping. I would say specifically "tactical strategy game". The other thing that I find missing are some of the cool things that you actually do. Be specific with your verbs. For example: play as a famous gangster from history, storm into speakeasies, kill crime bosses. All those things are really neat. I especially love killing crime bosses. Why don't they say that in the short description? Tell me what I'm literally going to be doing in the game.

I think the basic problem that I have with both of these short descriptions is that they lean too heavily on describing setting and mood. Both of those can be evoked better by screenshots and video. And if you spend too long describing the mood and the setting, you don't have enough time to talk about the gameplay. What are the verbs? What do I do in this game? What is the genre? The short description is really the best place to describe those things, and so it should do the heavy lifting.

Now let me show you a couple of examples that I really do like. The first one is from the building game Forager: "Forager, the highly popular and quirky idle game that you want to actively keep playing. Explore, craft, gather, and manage resources. Find secrets and build your base out of nothing. Buy land to explore and expand." This is a great short description. It has very specific verbs: explore, craft, gather, manage resources, find secrets, build a base. All these things are very good. It also includes social proof, such as "highly popular".

Let's look at another one. Thrill of the Fight, virtual reality boxing: "The Thrill of the Fight is a room-scale only VR boxing game focused on authenticity. Face off in the virtual ring where you'll jab, dodge, and sweat your way to the top of the boxing world. Grab your gloves, step through the ropes, and become a champion." What I really like about this is it sets the genre and it sets your expectations. Look here: room-scale VR. And it also defines the sub-genre, boxing, but "focused on authenticity". This isn't a game like Mike Tyson's Punch-Out. It's more of a simulation boxing game. Being that specific helps get the right audience to like your game. It also has great verbs that show what you do in this game: jab, dodge, sweat. I hope there's some sort of sweat meter in the game. I haven't actually played it, but I like the verbs. Some critiques I'd have: I do think you should be a little bit more specific. It says all these high-level verbs, but do I upgrade my character? Do I have to go on a roguelike adventure? Do I have to level up my HP? Do I have to craft boxing gloves? It helps to be specific about what you do round to round.

Let's look at the next one, Dead Cells: "Dead Cells is a roguelike, Metroidvania-inspired action platformer. You'll explore a sprawling, ever-changing castle, assuming you're able to fight your way past its keepers in 2D souls-like combat. No checkpoints. Kill, die, learn, repeat." Here's what I really like about this one. It covers the genre: roguelike, Metroidvania-inspired action platformer. Very specific. I know those are very technical terms, but you're trying to sell to technical-loving people. The people who play Metroidvanias are going to love to see that that's in there. It also shows off great verbs: explore, fight, kill, die, learn, repeat. All those really exciting things really bring out what you do in this game. It also sets the expectation: it's 2D souls-like combat, no checkpoints. It's saying this is a hard game. You don't want to make a hard game seem like a nice, easy, casual game. So be very clear about your difficulty.

Those are just a few examples of what I like in short descriptions. Next, I'm going to show you how to write a short description.
