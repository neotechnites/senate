The idea for my game comes from an old Halo Reach custom game called Duck Hunt.    Halo Reach Duck Hunt custom game lobby / menu
The idea of that game was 15 ducks are all running through an obstacle course while someone with a view of all of them shoots at them so they can't complete it.    Duck Hunt from the sniper's POV

This idea and concept to me felt like it could sustain a full game and be completely fleshed out, but no one has. For those who don't know, a panopticon is an architectural design for a prison. The idea is that you have a tower in the middle that can see every cell. But from the cell's perspective, you never truly know if a guard is watching you.    Original Bentham panopticon drawing over Duck Hunt duck-POV gameplay
What this does is create the feeling, for all prisoners, of constantly being watched, whether they are or not, letting just a few guards staff big prisons.    A second panopticon image over the same Duck Hunt gameplay
    
Panopticon is a first -person, asymmetric multiplayer game about escaping a prison built so one guard can see everyone. One friend sits in the tower with a sniper rifle, everyone else runs the ring for the portal. And the other prisoners aren't your team. You sprint between cover, shove each other into the line of fire, and whoever makes it out takes the rifle and the tower.    Our gameplay: sniper POV in the tower, then runner POV on the lane, then a runner reaching the portal
Now, that sounds like exactly the kind of game I'm about to complain about. A little party friend -slop game. You play it for three hours with your friends, you laugh, and you never open it again. And that's the problem. Because I think that's what indie multiplayer has become, and I think it loses that absolute magic of needing to spend real time in a game to play all its content and unlock all its content. Nowadays, all content is available from being opened, and you get through all of it in three hours.    Gameplay footage from my game 9/11
