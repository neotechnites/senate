## Why River Ruins reads as a song (measured, 90 s from its main section at ~0:36)
Compared against 11 YET soundsystem tracks he called ambient (SIBERIA, HYDRO, DECOY, 2002 TOKYO, TIME SPLITTERS, SKYRING, BLUE STREAM and slowed versions):
- **Steady beat:** pulse clarity is 0.78, against 0.41 for YET. The breakbeat locks into a backbeat instead of skittering.
- **Fuller mid-range:** 17% of the energy sits at 300 Hz to 3 kHz, against 9% for YET. YET puts 84 to 93% of its energy in the sub bass. River Ruins carries a lead or chords in the mids that you can follow.
- **Sustained energy after the drop:** the 2 s level varies by 1.6 dB (sd), against 3.8 dB for YET. Beatless or dropped-out windows make up 2% of the time, against 14%. The level is 2.5 dB hotter.
- **Hook repetition:** self-similarity at the 8 to 16 bar lag is 0.61, against 0.55.
- Chroma clarity is about the same (0.22 against 0.23). The tonal content isn't what separates them. What does is a steady groove with mids underneath it.

Method: 37 SoundCloud accounts. Each track's top 60 by plays plus 20 most recent were pulled (top 30 for the later artists because of a SoundCloud rate limit). DJ mixes over 12 min, remixes, feat./vocal titles, slowed or sped-up versions, clips and showcases were dropped. 1,180 tracks were analysed. Each track gets a weighted z-score distance to River Ruins on 12 features. Main-section time is the first point where level, sub and drum density all hold near the track's peak for 8 s or more (2 s resolution). There are at most 3 tracks per artist. All links returned 200 from SoundCloud oEmbed.
